# The Good Law List

The Llama Award is computed over the **Good Law List**: instruments that repeal democracy-destroying
law, or add democracy-repairing law.

## The admission tests

An instrument gets on the list by satisfying at least one of eight tests, and the record says which:

**repeal** · **restore** · **transparency** · **accountability** · **franchise** · **due_process** ·
**speech** · **purse**

These are structural tests about the mechanism of self-government, not policy preferences. That is
what makes the list usable by citizens who disagree with each other about almost everything — and it is
checked out loud every time something is proposed, because the temptation to slip a preferred policy
onto a list of democratic repairs is constant.

## Each item carries

* a **frozen key**,
* a **goodness rating, 1 to 100** — how much repair it delivers, which is its weight in the arithmetic,
* a **citizen goodness vote**, which **orders the list and moves no rating**.

That last distinction is the same one the problem side makes, for the same reason. Citizens should
control what the movement looks at first. Letting a popular vote quietly reweight a published number
about a named living person would turn the arithmetic into a poll.

## The full library

The Good Law List is the assessed subset of the [Good Laws](../good_laws/overview.mdx) library, which
records every one of these instruments and what happened to it — passed, voted down, or never allowed
a vote at all.
