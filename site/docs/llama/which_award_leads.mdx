# Which award leads

Every politician with an in-office record carries **both** awards. A card has room to lead with one.
The rule that picks it is called **dominance**.

## The rule

The **dominant** award is the one that says more about this record — the one further from nothing. A
person with a 71% Monkey and a 4% Llama leads with the Monkey. A person with an 8% Monkey and a 66%
Llama leads with the Llama.

The other award does not disappear. It renders beside it, always, on the record page.

## Why not just show both everywhere

Because a card in a list has one line, and a citizen scanning a roster of a hundred names needs the
most informative fact first. Showing two numbers of equal visual weight in a small space reliably
produces the wrong reading — people take the first one they see as the summary regardless of which it
is.

## What dominance is not

* It is not a **verdict**. It picks which number leads; it does not merge them, average them, or
  produce a third number that ranks the person overall. There is no overall.
* It does not **hide** the other award. Any page with room shows both.
* It does not depend on **party, cohort, or era**. It is a comparison between two numbers about one
  record.

## Both, side by side

Some people carry a voting-record award *and* a [Flamingo](../flamingo/overview.mdx) state — a former
officeholder running again, for example. Those render side by side as well, and the wall between them
holds: the voting record feeds Monkey and Llama, everything else feeds the Flamingo, and neither ever
contaminates the other.
