# We The Citizens S

:::tip[The question this area answers]
**I am a Socialist — is this site for me?**
:::

Yes. **WeCitizensSocialism.com** is the Socialist front door onto We The Citizens.
It asks you for nothing up front and shows you one thing: **your own seats, the
people holding them right now, what they actually voted for, and who could replace
them.**

This page is the plain-text reference for the edition. The front door itself is
[We The Citizens S](./overview.mdx).

There is a mirror of this page for the party you actually argue with —
[We The Citizens L](../l/reference_text.mdx) — and the two are identical in every
respect except the party label. That symmetry is the point, and it is enforced,
not promised. See [The mirror rule](#the-mirror-rule) below.

## Why a Socialist front door exists

The main app at [app.WeTheCitizens.io](https://app.WeTheCitizens.io) asks a citizen
to reason from first principles about the social contract. That is the right ask,
and it is a big one for somebody who arrived from an argument thirty seconds ago.
It also asks you to look at problems and politicians without a partisan frame —
which, however correct, reads to a committed partisan as *"this site is for the
other team."*

A Socialist reading WeCitizensD.com is being asked to accept a frame that is not
theirs — the same ask the main app makes, wearing a blue chip. Neither R nor D is
"the closest of the two" for you. You are served by a door of your own, and by an
honesty column pointed at the party you actually argue with.

So this edition lowers the first ask to one sentence:

> **Here are your elected officials. Here is what they actually voted for. Here is
> who could replace them.**

Cross-party persuasion is expensive and slow. **In-party accountability is cheap
and fast**, because you already agree with the party label and only have to be
shown that *this particular officeholder* is not delivering on it. Holding your own
side to its own stated standard is the highest-leverage act available to a voter.

## What you see on the front page

The front page is a stack of **seat blocks**, one for each seat you actually vote
on — your two US senators, your US House seat, your governor, your state assembly
seat. Every block has the same three columns:

| Column | Who is in it |
| --- | --- |
| **In office** | The person holding the seat right now, with their record |
| **Socialist challenger** | The leading challenger on your own side — a *primary* challenger when the incumbent is also a Socialist |
| **Libertarian challenger** | The leading challenger on the side you actually argue with, same photo size, same arithmetic |

The columns never reorder, and an empty column stays a column — you get an honest
*"no vetted Socialist challenger yet for this seat"* and a way to recommend
somebody, never a quietly collapsed grid.

Inside each column: the incumbent's [Monkey Award](../monkey/overview.mdx) and
[Llama Award](../llama/overview.mdx), a challenger's Qualified and Vetted bars, and
the specific sourced votes underneath. Every number comes from
[Voting Records](../voting_records/overview.mdx) and
[Qualifications](../qualifications/overview.mdx) — the same pipeline, run
party-blind, that produces every other number on this site.

## The mirror rule

Partisan skins become partisan arguments unless something stops them. This is what
stops them:

1. **One award pipeline.** Awards are computed once, party-blind. No edition
   re-weights, re-rounds, or re-thresholds anything.
2. **Symmetric harshness.** A Socialist incumbent at 71% on the Monkey Award reads
   exactly as a Libertarian incumbent at 71% does on the L edition. There is one set
   of strings, parameterized by the party label.
3. **No comparative copy.** Nothing on any edition compares the parties,
   characterizes the other party, or attributes motive to a party. **The unit of
   judgement is a person and a vote.**
4. **The cross-party column is the honesty check.** An edition that only ever
   showed its own team's challengers would be a party organ. Showing the good
   person on the other side, same size, same arithmetic, is what makes this a
   citizens' tool instead of a campaign tool.
5. **A test enforces it, once per pair.** One seat, rendered under both editions of
   a pair, must produce the same page once the labels are swapped.

The one-line review test for any change proposed here: *would I ship the exact
mirror of this to the paired edition tomorrow?* If not, it does not ship.

## Four doors, two pairs

The four doors are **two pairs, not four unrelated skins**, and the pairing is the
whole reason this scales without becoming four editorial voices. **R** and **D**
are each other's reflection on the **partisan axis**. **L** and **S** are each
other's reflection on the **economic axis** — a Libertarian's argument is with a
Socialist and vice versa, which is the same relationship R and D have with each
other. S is paired with **L** across the economic axis.

A door is only ever added when it comes with a partner on the same axis, because
the cross-party column has to be filled by *somebody* and the mirror check needs a
counterpart to run against. A door proposed without a partner is not a door; it is
an unpaired skin with nothing to check it against.

| Edition | Front door | Audience | Paired with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **R** | [WeCitizensR.com](https://wecitizensr.com) | Republicans | D — partisan axis |
| **D** | [WeCitizensD.com](https://wecitizensd.com) | Democrats | R — partisan axis |
| **L** | [WeCitizensL.com](https://wecitizensl.com) | Libertarians | S — economic axis |
| **S** | [WeCitizensSocialism.com](https://wecitizenssocialism.com) | Socialists | L — economic axis |

## One product, four doors

This is not a second product and not a second deployment. It is **the same web app,
the same data, and the same admin-approved records**, rendered differently because
of the domain the request arrived on. The roster, the awards, the roll-call
evidence and the proposed laws are all the one shared corpus. The edition is a
request attribute, never a second server and never a second data set.

Reading here is wide open — no account, no sign-in, nothing recorded about you.
Every *action* (vote, recommend, back a challenger, help vet one) hands you to
[app.WeTheCitizens.io](https://app.WeTheCitizens.io) to join, and brings you back to
the seat you were looking at.
