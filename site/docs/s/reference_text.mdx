# We The Citizens S

:::tip[The question this area answers]
**I am a Democratic Socialist — is this site for me?**
:::

Yes. **WeCitizensSocialism.com** is the Democratic Socialist front door onto We The Citizens.
It asks you for nothing up front and shows you one thing: **your own seats, the
people holding them right now, what they actually voted for, and who could replace
them.**

This page is the plain-text reference for the edition. The front door itself is
[We The Citizens S](./overview.mdx).

This door has no partner edition and no mirror. What belongs on it is decided by
`marketing/messaging/socialism.md` and by nothing else — the other three doors are
written for their own audiences and constrain nothing here. See
[What this door owes the others](#what-this-door-owes-the-others) below.

## Why a Democratic Socialist front door exists

The main app at [app.WeTheCitizens.io](https://app.WeTheCitizens.io) asks a citizen
to reason from first principles about the social contract. That is the right ask,
and it is a big one for somebody who arrived from an argument thirty seconds ago.
It also asks you to look at problems and politicians without a partisan frame —
which, however correct, reads to a committed partisan as *"this site is for the
other team."*

A Democratic Socialist arriving at somebody else's door is being asked to accept a frame that is not
theirs. You are served by a door of your own — written for this community, from this
community's own messaging file — and by a challenger column that stays honest by showing
the strongest candidate for the seat wherever they come from.

So this edition lowers the first ask to one sentence:

> **Here are your elected officials. Here is what they actually voted for. Here is
> who could replace them.**

Cross-party persuasion is expensive and slow. **In-party accountability is cheap
and fast**, because you already agree with the party label and only have to be
shown that *this particular officeholder* is not delivering on it. Holding your own
side to its own stated standard is the highest-leverage act available to a voter.

## What you see on the front page

The front page is a stack of **seat blocks**, one for each seat you actually vote
on — your two US senators, your US House seat, your governor, your state assembly
seat. Every block has the same three columns:

| Column | Who is in it |
| --- | --- |
| **In office** | The person holding the seat right now, with their record |
| **Democratic Socialist challenger** | The leading challenger on your own side — a *primary* challenger when the incumbent is also a Democratic Socialist |
| **Challengers from other parties** | The strongest challenger for this seat who is not from your side, whatever party that turns out to be — same photo size, same arithmetic |

The columns never reorder, and an empty column stays a column — you get an honest
*"no vetted Democratic Socialist challenger yet for this seat"* and a way to recommend
somebody, never a quietly collapsed grid.

Inside each column: the incumbent's [Monkey Award](../monkey/overview.mdx) and
[Llama Award](../llama/overview.mdx), a challenger's Qualified and Vetted bars, and
the specific sourced votes underneath. Every number comes from
[Voting Records](../voting_records/overview.mdx) and
[Qualifications](../qualifications/overview.mdx) — the same pipeline, run
party-blind, that produces every other number on this site.

## What this door owes the others

Nothing, editorially. There is **no mirror rule and no pairing**. The four doors were
once locked together in two pairs — R with D, L with S — and every door was required
to be its partner's reflection with the party label swapped. That rule was removed on
2026-08-21. This page can be improved, cut or rewritten without owing the same change
to any other edition, and no door needs a partner in order to exist.

What still binds every edition is the **arithmetic**, not the prose:

1. **One award pipeline.** Awards are computed once, party-blind. No edition
   re-weights, re-rounds, or re-thresholds anything.
2. **The same number means the same thing everywhere.** A Democratic Socialist incumbent at 71% on
   the Monkey Award earned it exactly the way anyone else at 71% did. The number comes
   from the shared pipeline; only the audience it is being explained to is different.
3. **No comparative copy.** Nothing on any edition compares the parties,
   characterizes another party, or attributes motive to a party. **The unit of
   judgement is a person and a vote.** This is the one editorial rule that survived,
   and it survived on its own merits — it is what keeps a partisan door from becoming
   a partisan argument.
4. **Cross-party challengers are always shown.** An edition that only ever showed its
   own team's challengers would be a party organ. The strongest challenger from
   outside your side gets the same photo size and the same arithmetic.

The review test for a change proposed here is no longer *"would I ship the mirror of
this to the paired edition?"* It is: **does `marketing/messaging/socialism.md` support
this, and does it judge a person and a vote rather than a party?**

## One product, four doors

This is not a second product and not a second deployment. It is **the same web app,
the same data, and the same admin-approved records**, rendered differently because
of the domain the request arrived on. The roster, the awards, the roll-call
evidence and the proposed laws are all the one shared corpus. The edition is a
request attribute, never a second server and never a second data set.

Reading here is wide open — no account, no sign-in, nothing recorded about you.
Every *action* (vote, recommend, back a challenger, help vet one) hands you to
[app.WeTheCitizens.io](https://app.WeTheCitizens.io) to join, and brings you back to
the seat you were looking at.
