# Florida HB 3 — Anti-ESG Legislation (2023)

* **Law:** Florida House Bill 3, amending Chapter 215 and related Florida Statutes
* **Passed:** May 2023 (special legislative session)
* **Vote:** Florida Senate 28–9; Florida House 80–31
* **Signed by:** Governor Ron DeSantis
* **Part of:** DeSantis's broader "anti-woke" legislative agenda

## What It Does

HB 3 is one of the most sweeping state anti-ESG laws passed to date. Its provisions include:

* **Investment mandate** — Requires the State Board of Administration (SBA), which manages ~$230 billion in state pension and investment funds, to make investment decisions **solely** on financial return and risk — explicitly prohibiting consideration of ESG factors such as environmental impact, social metrics, or governance ratings.
* **Contract restrictions** — Restricts state agencies from entering contracts with companies that use ESG criteria in their own business decisions in ways deemed to conflict with Florida's interests.
* **Boycott prohibitions** — Prohibits state entities from doing business with firms that boycott certain industries (energy, firearms, agriculture) for ESG-related reasons.
* **Financial institution disclosure** — Requires banks and financial institutions that want to do business with Florida government to certify they do not engage in politically disfavored ESG practices.
* **Local government preemption** — Prohibits Florida local governments from adopting ESG investment standards.

## Why Critics Oppose It

* **Government dictating private investment philosophy** — Telling a private fund manager or bank what analysis they may or may not apply to investment decisions is an unprecedented intrusion into private financial judgment.
* **Punishes political expression** — Companies that have publicly committed to ESG frameworks are penalized for that commitment when seeking state contracts — punishing their expressive conduct.
* **Fiduciary malpractice** — Many institutional investors argue that ignoring climate risk, governance factors, and social risk is not neutral investing — it is investing with a politically mandated blind spot that exposes retirees to unaccounted risk.
* **Viewpoint discrimination** — The law disfavors one set of investment values (ESG/environmental concern) while explicitly favoring another (fossil fuel, firearms industries). That is government taking sides in a values debate and penalizing the disfavored side.
* **Financial costs** — As with Texas SB 13, Florida's anti-ESG push led major Wall Street firms to withdraw from or reduce participation in Florida municipal bond underwriting, raising borrowing costs for state and local governments.
* **Chilling effect on speech** — Companies modify their public communications and internal policies to avoid triggering the law, even where their underlying investment judgment remains unchanged.

## Constitutional Concerns

* 1st Amendment — government penalizing private companies for expressive conduct (ESG commitments, public sustainability pledges) as a condition of public contracts.
* Compelled speech — requiring companies to certify compliance with state-preferred political positions on industry as a condition of doing business with the state.
* Dormant Commerce Clause — state regulation of out-of-state companies' investment practices distorts interstate commerce.

## Key Votes to Screen

Florida state legislature. Senate: 28–9. House: 80–31. Relevant for screening Florida state legislators.

Nationally, this law is a template. Track which federal legislators praise or co-sponsor federal versions of anti-ESG legislation (several have been introduced in Congress). Any member endorsing federal anti-ESG mandates is endorsing government control over private investment decisions.

## Why This Matters for We The Citizens

HB 3 is part of a pattern: government using its purchasing power to punish companies for their political and economic speech. Whether the favored industry is fossil fuels, firearms, or Israel, the mechanism is the same. Consistency demands that politicians who object to BDS laws on free-speech grounds also object to anti-ESG laws — and vice versa. The inconsistency is the tell.
