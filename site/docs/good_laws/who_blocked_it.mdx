# Who blocked it

Saying a named living person stopped a bill is the most consequential claim on this site. It is also
the most easily done badly. The rules below are the ones that make it publishable.

## State the act, not the character

The record says **what a person did**: held the bill at a named stage, on a named date, by a named
mechanism. It does not say what they *are*, what they *wanted*, or who they were *working for*. Motive
is an argument; procedure is a record.

## Every element carries its own source

Not one citation for the paragraph — a citation for the act, and a citation for the stage. Procedural
history is public and it is boring to gather, and the boredom is the entire reason it works: nobody
can dispute a committee calendar.

## Where the record is thin, it says so

Sometimes it is genuinely known that a bill was held and not reliably known by whom. That is recorded
as a **gap** — the bill died at this stage, and we cannot attribute it — rather than being attributed
to whoever was most convenient. A named gap is a real finding. It is also an invitation: it tells a
citizen with access to a local archive exactly what would close it.

## If you are the subject

There is a correction channel on every record, and a correction from the subject of the record is the
correction the movement wants most. Your submission is labelled as coming from you, and it is
**not** refused for that reason. Staff can file on your behalf; the delegation shows as unverified
until it is confirmed, and the correction is accepted meanwhile — because requiring the paperwork
first means publishing something wrong for longer than necessary.
