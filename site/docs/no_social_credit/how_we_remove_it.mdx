# How we remove it

Three requirements. Each is narrow, each is testable, and each becomes actual legislative language
rather than a principle.

## 1. Due process where consequence lands

If a decision cuts off a person's ability to bank, earn or travel, they get **the reason and a real
appeal** — to somebody who did not make the original decision, on a clock, in writing.

Note what this does not require: it does not require anybody to serve anybody. It requires that when
ordinary participation is removed, the removal is explained and reviewable.

## 2. No inferred score without inspection

A person can **see, and correct, what is held about them** — including scores assembled by inference
from data they never handed over. Inspection without correction is a courtesy; correction is the
requirement.

## 3. No essential access revoked by unpublished rule

If the rule cannot be read, it cannot be complied with, and a rule nobody can comply with is not a
rule — it is a discretion. Publication is the minimum condition under which the word "policy" means
anything.

## Where these become law

As bill text with numbered sections and a repeal list, published in the open and waiting for a

## Our own karma score, and the honest comparison

This site keeps a **[Karma](../karma/overview.mdx)** score for citizens. Rather than dodge the
obvious question, here is the difference, stated as four properties:

* **It only ever goes up, from things you chose to do** and logged yourself. Nothing is inferred
  about you and nothing is collected behind you.
* **It cannot take anything away.** No account, no service, no access. There is nothing to be cut off
  from.
* **It is voluntary and leaveable.** The product runs fully on your own machine, with no account at
  all — see **[Get Started](../get_started/overview.mdx)**.
* **It is published with its derivation**, so you can check the arithmetic on yourself.

A score you opt into, that only adds, and that you can walk away from is not the same object as a
score assigned to you that can close your bank account.

**If ours ever acquired the power to take something away, it would belong on the problem list with
the rest** — and we would rather write that sentence now, in public, than be asked about it later.
