# The American version

Ask an American whether they would accept a [social credit system](/docs/no_social_credit/overview/) and the answer is no, across every
party. Ask whether they have ever been cut off from something — an account, a platform, a service —
with no stated reason and no route of appeal, and the answers change.

## What it actually is

Not one government registry. A **patchwork of private and quasi-public gatekeepers** that between
them decide what a person can do:

* **De-banking and payment cut-off.** An account closed, the reason withheld, no appeal. Losing
  access to payments is losing access to employment, housing and most of ordinary life.
* **Platform and marketplace removal.** Access to reach people, or to earn, revoked under a rule the
  person is not permitted to read.
* **Scoring by inference.** Assessments assembled from data a person never handed over and cannot
  inspect or correct. They do not have to be accurate to have consequences.
* **Access conditioned on compliance.** A service that is effectively mandatory, granted or withheld
  on terms nobody voted on.

## The definition that matters

What makes something a social credit system is not its branding.

> **Consequences assigned to a person by an unaccountable process, with no due process attached.**

By that definition, the question is not whether America could get one. It is how much of one is
already assembled — and the honest answer is: quite a lot of it, built piece by piece by parties who
each only built one piece.

## The worked example: vehicles

The clearest current case, because it is physical and documented as a named problem:

* **Vehicle surveillance** — a car that continuously reports where it has been, to parties the owner
  did not choose.
* **Remote shutdown** — the capability to disable a vehicle from outside it.
* **Older vehicles regulated off the road** — removing the option of driving something that can do
  neither of the above.

Put the three together and you have **a permission to travel that can be revoked**. Each piece
arrived separately with its own justification, which is exactly how the rest of it arrived too. The
files are in **[Problems](../problems/overview.mdx)**.
