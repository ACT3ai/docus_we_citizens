# Report a problem law

Anyone can report a problem law. You do not need an account to start, and you do not need permission
to be right.

## What you submit

The form asks for four things:

1. **The law** — name and citation.
2. **What it does** — in your own words, in plain language.
3. **Which root cause it creates** — the specific harm, named. If you can name more than one, name
   them all.
4. **Sources.** These are **mandatory on this kind of submission**, enforced when you submit and not
   later. Problem laws are the hardest records in the movement to source and the most consequential
   to get wrong, so the requirement sits at the door.

## What happens to it

Your submission lands in a review queue as a **request, never a record**. Nothing you send is
published until it is reviewed — an unreviewed submission is never rendered publicly, because a claim
about how the country is governed should not appear on a public site because one person typed it.

From there, one of three things happens, and you are told which:

* **Approved** — a draft problem law record is created from your submission and enters the library.
* **Sent back for changes** — with a stated reason you can read.
* **Declined** — also with a stated reason, shown to you verbatim.

A movement that silently drops citizen contributions stops receiving them. Nothing is ever
hard-deleted: what the movement was asked and chose not to do is part of its record too.

## If you are not signed in

The controls still render normally. Clicking one takes you to sign up, remembers what you were doing,
and returns you to it when you are done. It is an invitation, not a wall.
