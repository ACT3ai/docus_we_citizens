# The action catalog

## Challengers — putting better people in seats

Identify somebody promising. Vet them against the published rubrics. Recruit them. Support a filing.
And, at the top of the scale by a wide margin, help one win.

This track carries the heaviest weights in the catalog because it is the only one that changes who
holds the seat. Also here: **flagging a worrisome challenger**, which carries the same weight as
identifying a good one and a far heavier sourcing burden, because it is a negative public claim about a
living person.

## Communications — making the argument reach people

Public posts, media appearances, press pickups, and **published explainers**.

Explainers are weighted well above posts on purpose. A post is a moment; an explainer is a thing that
keeps working, that somebody can cite next year, and that reduces the number of people who have to
rediscover the same argument from scratch.

## Organizing — the work that leaves no paper trail

Meetings held, citizens recruited, canvass shifts, poll-watching shifts.

This is the track most likely to be undercounted, because most of it produces no artifact. It leans
hardest on **peer reporting** — one citizen reporting another citizen's work — for exactly that reason.

## Lawmaking — feeding the libraries

Proposing a problem, reporting a problem law, drafting or improving a fix bill, closing a coverage gap
nobody had closed.

The highest-leverage work in the catalog for somebody with an evening and a good archive.

## Participation — keeping the record alive

Adding a politician video or channel, adding a source, filing a correction, transcribing.

Individually small, and the reason the rest of the product has anything to read. A record that nobody
feeds becomes wrong quietly.

## Picking one

**One hour:** add a channel or a video for somebody whose record is thin, or file a correction on a row
you know is wrong.

**One evening:** close a named coverage gap, or write an explainer for the argument you keep having.

**One month:** vet a challenger properly, against the rubrics, and publish what you found.

**One cycle:** recruit somebody worth electing.
