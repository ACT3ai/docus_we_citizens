# Ana Kasparian

* **X:** [@AnaKasparian](https://x.com/AnaKasparian) (~887K followers)
* **Status:** Alive (born July 7, 1986, Los Angeles, CA)
* **Role:** Journalist, political commentator, co-host and executive producer of The Young Turks (TYT); host of *The Point with Ana Kasparian* and *No Filter with Ana Kasparian*; Substack newsletter *Unaligned*

## Background

Full name: Anahit Misak "Ana" Kasparian. Daughter of Armenian immigrants; her paternal great-grandparents survived the 1915 Armenian Genocide. Raised in Reseda (San Fernando Valley). Armenian was her first language. Trained as a ballet dancer from age 3 to 19.

Education: B.A. in Journalism, California State University Northridge (CSUN), 2007; M.A. in Political Science, CSUN, 2010. Taught journalism courses part-time at CSUN from 2013.

Career started at CBS Radio (KFWB and KNX) before joining TYT in April 2007 as a fill-in producer. Quickly became co-host and executive producer. TYT grew to billions of views under her involvement. She also contributed to MSNBC, CNN, and RT; wrote for The Raw Story; delivered a TEDx talk in 2016 on campaign finance reform; and represented YouTube creators at the Munich Security Conference.

In 2025, joined *Her Take*, a cross-ideological all-women panel show (with Jillian Michaels and Lindy Li) hosted by Patrick Bet-David.

**Political evolution:** Registered Democrat 2004–2024. Now describes herself as "politically unaligned" and "politically homeless." Criticizes identity politics, cancel culture, soft-on-crime policies, California Democrats (especially Newsom), and gender-affirming care for minors. Has praised some Republican actions. Still opposes corporate capture, foreign wars, and purity-test politics. Maintains the *Unaligned* Substack (tens of thousands of subscribers) documenting this realignment.

**Personal:** Married to Christian Lopez (filmmaker, minor league baseball) since November 2015. No children; has stated she does not plan to have any. Instagram @anakasparianofficial (~1M followers).

## Notes

**Israel/Gaza:** Kasparian is one of the most sustained progressive critics of Israel in US independent media. She has publicly called Israel's actions in Gaza "genocide" and "evil," accused Israel of dragging the US into wars, stated "Israel is killing America," and criticized US aid. She links her criticism to her Armenian heritage — noting Israel armed Azerbaijan during the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh conflict, which she connects to the ethnic cleansing of Armenians.

She has debated pro-Israel voices including Bill Maher, Candace Owens, Shabbos Kestenbaum, and Avital Leibovich. In February 2026, she posted an X thread praising Tucker Carlson on avoiding war with Iran and responded to critics with a direct reference to "the goyim waking up." She stood by the comment and denied antisemitism, framing her position as policy-based opposition to war. The comment drew significant backlash and reporting.

**Personal costs:** She has reported income and job losses, harassment, and threats from individuals including IDF soldiers who allegedly sent her weapon images. She has stated the costs were substantial and has continued speaking anyway — this is the clearest evidence for criteria 4 and 9.

**AIPAC/donor capture:** She names AIPAC and donor-controlled politicians by name as a regular part of her commentary, giving her strong scores on criteria 6 and 7.

**Executive experience:** As executive producer of TYT — a major independent media operation with a global audience, substantial staff, and complex production — she qualifies on criterion 11. This is outside-government executive experience, which the criteria explicitly credit.

**Low scores:** She has a minimal public record on US intelligence services (criteria 5 and 8), and no government experience (criterion 13). Economic literacy (criterion 15) is present but not a primary focus.

**Armenian background as a lens:** Her family's genocide history is not incidental. It is the lens through which she evaluates Israel, the US government's handling of foreign conflicts, and the pattern of powerful nations denying atrocities. This gives her public commentary on foreign policy unusual depth and personal stakes.
