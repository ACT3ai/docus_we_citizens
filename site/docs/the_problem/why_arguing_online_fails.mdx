# Why arguing online fails

Almost everyone who arrives here has already spent years doing the obvious thing: reading, getting
angry, and arguing about it in public. It is worth being precise about why that produces so little,
because the answer determines what the alternative has to look like.

## Four reasons

**It has no memory.** You establish something on Tuesday and re-establish it on Thursday to a different
person. Nothing accumulates. A thread is not a record — it cannot be cited, corrected, or built on, and
in a year it is gone.

**It has no shared yardstick.** Two people can be arguing about "corruption" while meaning entirely
different things, and there is nothing to appeal to that either of them accepts.

**It rewards the wrong thing.** Engagement rewards the sharpest phrasing, which is almost never the
most careful. The most valuable contribution available — *"here is my chain, here are the three links I
am missing, here is my threshold"* — performs terribly and always will.

**It ends at agreement.** Even total victory produces two people who now agree, and no change to
anything.

## What the alternative needs

**Persistence.** A record that outlives the argument, that a stranger can cite, and that can be
corrected rather than restated.

**A fixed standard.** Something both sides are measured against that neither wrote.

**A visible chain.** Reasoning laid out so it can be attacked at a specific link instead of dismissed
wholesale.

**An action at the end of it.** A place where the conclusion turns into something done — and a record
that it was done.

That list is, more or less, the product specification. It is why the app has a public data repository
rather than a comment section, a social contract as the reference standard, evidence chains with
explicit gaps, and an action catalog with a log.

## The honest caveat

The movement is not against public argument, and it runs a social network. Arguing is how people find
each other, and the paired network exists so a fix somebody reasoned through can be published, read,
and built on.

The claim here is narrower: **argument is the on-ramp, not the work.** A movement whose only artifact
is a timeline has produced nothing that survives the week.
