# Propose a correction

Two routes, and both of them work.

## Route one: through the app

Every record carries a feedback or correction control. Point at the specific row, say what is wrong,
and cite the public record if you have it. You do not need an account to start — the control renders
normally, the click takes you to sign up, and you come back to where you were with your work intact.

## Route two: through git

It is a public repository. Read the files, fix the row, and send it back the way you would to any open
source project.

## A proposal is a request, not a record

Your submission lands in an inbox. It does not touch the movement's data. **Approval is the only thing
that crosses that line**, it is admin-only, and it works identically whether the app is running on your
laptop or on a server.

Until it is reviewed, it is not rendered publicly. Nothing about a living person appears on this site
because one person typed it.

## A decline states its reason, and you see it verbatim

This is a rule, not a courtesy.

**A movement that silently drops citizen contributions stops receiving them.** A refusal somebody can
read is still somebody who was heard, and they will file again. A submission that vanishes teaches
exactly one lesson, and it is the wrong one.

So every decline and every request for changes carries a **mandatory stated reason**, and the author is
shown it in the reviewer's own words. Your submissions page shows what happened to each one, declines
included.

## Nothing is hard-deleted

What the movement was asked and chose **not** to do is part of its record too. A movement that keeps
only its accepted contributions has edited its own history, which is the thing it exists to complain
about.

## Self-interest is labelled, never blocked

If you have a stake — you are the subject, you work in the agency, you are a politician or their staff
— say so and file anyway. The submission is flagged and reviewed on its merits. The movement does not
refuse information because of who it came from; it refuses to hide who it came from.
