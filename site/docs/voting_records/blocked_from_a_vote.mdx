# Blocked from a vote

## The act

Somebody kept an instrument from advancing. Held in committee. Denied a rule. Left off the calendar.
Stripped in conference. Run out of session.

The outcome is identical to defeating it. The record is blank.

## Why a blank is not nothing

This is the exact structure of [the deadlock pattern](../the_problem/the_deadlock_pattern.mdx), applied
to procedure. The absence of a roll call gets read as the absence of an event — so the most
consequential decision available in the system is also the least visible one.

Treating that blank as *nothing happened* is how the least accountable act in the process became the
safest one to perform.

## So it is recorded as an event

* **Who** held it.
* **At what stage** — committee, rule, calendar, conference.
* **By what mechanism.**
* **A source for each of those.**

Not one citation for the paragraph. A citation for the act, and a citation for the stage.

## Why it weighs about thirty floor votes

The single most consequential weight in the product, and it deserves its argument stated rather than
asserted.

**A floor vote is one voice among hundreds**, cast in public, with a roll call anybody can look up. It
is accountable by construction: a citizen who dislikes it can see it and act on it at the next
election.

**Keeping a bill off the floor is one person deciding for everyone**, in a room, with no roll call and
often no record that a decision occurred at all. Largest effect, least accountability, and no trace for
a citizen to respond to.

Weighting the two equally would mean the arithmetic **rewards moving your damage into the room where
nobody can see it**. So it does not.

## Where it lands

Blocking a repair is heavily penalised in the [Llama Award](../llama/overview.mdx). Blocking a problem
law is credited. Same verb, opposite sides — which is why the classifier has twelve categories rather
than four.

## Where we cannot attribute it

Sometimes a bill is known to have died at a stage and not reliably known by whose decision. That is
recorded as a **gap** — died here, unattributed — never assigned to whoever is most convenient.

It is also an open invitation. A citizen with access to a local archive can close it.
