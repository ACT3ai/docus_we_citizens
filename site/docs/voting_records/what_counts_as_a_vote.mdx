# What counts as a vote

## Three kinds of act

**A vote.** They voted, at a recorded stage, and the roll call says which way.

**An authorship.** They wrote or co-sponsored the instrument. This is a stronger claim about
responsibility than a vote — the instrument exists partly because of them.

**A block.** They kept something from advancing. → [Blocked from a vote](./blocked_from_a_vote.mdx)

## Stages, and why they are weighted

A vote is not one thing. An instrument passes through stages, and a vote at each carries a different
amount of information about the person casting it.

A committee vote is cast by a small number of people whose votes are decisive and less observed. A
floor vote is one among hundreds and heavily reported. Weighting them identically would misrepresent
both.

## Vehicles — recorded, and kept out of the arithmetic

Consequential provisions frequently arrive inside instruments nobody can vote against: a defence
authorisation, an appropriations package, a must-pass continuing resolution.

The vehicle class **is recorded** and shown to the reader, because a citizen deserves to know that a
yes vote was a yes on a package that also funded the government.

It **does not enter the equation**. The moment it does, every award becomes an argument about how much
benefit of the doubt a package earns — and that argument has no floor and no resolution. So: visible to
the reader, absent from the arithmetic.

## The classification

Every act is sorted into exactly one of twelve categories, formed by crossing what they did (vote /
author / block), which side the instrument is on (problem / good), the direction (advancing /
resisting), and where it happened (floor / the small room).

Twelve rather than four exists because of the **role-inversion trap**: the same verb means opposite
things depending on the instrument. Blocking a problem law is good. Blocking a repair is very bad. The
instrument's side is therefore part of the category rather than something inferred afterwards.

## Fact vs reading, one more time

Which way somebody voted is a fact. Whether that vote **advanced or resisted the harm** is the
movement's judgment — recorded in its own explicit field, so a reader can accept the first and reject
the second.
