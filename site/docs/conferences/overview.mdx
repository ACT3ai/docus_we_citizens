# Conferences

<CTA />

Every year a handful of big political conferences decide who gets the microphone in front of a party's
voters, and the organizers make that choice for them. We The Citizens runs the alternative: **one yearly
political strategy conference for each party, on that party's own site, where citizens vote on who
should speak.** This non-partisan site holds no conference of its own. It explains how they work and
sends you to yours.

## Why this exists

Citizens no longer assume that the long-serving Washington figure works for them, and they are right to
check. The same applies to the stage. When organizers hand out the microphone, the people on it are the
people the organizers chose, and the voters in the audience had no say in it.

These conferences turn that around. The people who speak are meant to be **intellectual and political
leaders who represent citizens**, and the citizens themselves say who that is. That includes the
**new political leaders** the movement is helping into office. They get a chance to speak, and citizens
get a chance to know them before they vote.

## One conference per party, and none here

Each party's conference belongs to that party's community, runs on that party's own site, and is
written in that community's own language. There is no movement-wide conference and no "all parties"
conference.

| Party | Conference |
|---|---|
| Republicans | [Yearly Conference on Political Right Strategy](../r/yearly_conference/overview.mdx), at WeCitizensR.com |
| Democrats | [The Yearly Strategic Conference](../d/yearly_conference/overview.mdx), at WeCitizensD.com |
| Libertarians | [Yearly Conference on Liberty Strategy](../l/yearly_conference/overview.mdx), at WeCitizensL.com |
| Democratic Socialists | [The Yearly Strategic Conference](../s/yearly_conference/overview.mdx), at WeCitizensSocialism.com |

This site's job is the one in the table: point you at yours. It is the same rule the movement applies to
everything partisan. See **[One App, Four Doors](../four_doors/overview.mdx)**.

## Citizens vote on the speakers

Every party's conference keeps a list of the people the movement is considering, and each one is put to
the citizens by name:

* **Yes or no, and how strongly.** Give a person **1 to 5 thumbs up** or **1 to 5 thumbs down**. A
  lukewarm yes and a firm yes are different answers, and both are recorded.
* **One vote per citizen per person.** Change your mind whenever you like. Only your latest answer
  counts.
* **A real person behind every vote.** You cast a vote signed in, with a confirmed email. Anyone can
  read the results.
* **Four numbers per person, in the open.** How many said yes, how strongly on average, how many said
  no, how strongly on average.

What citizens said is published as exactly that: **what citizens said**. It is never presented as a
judgment of the person. Voting is in alpha and is opening one party's conference at a time.

## The speaker list is the test

* **Judged on the record, not the reputation.** Every speaker's page shows what the movement can check
  about them: their **[Flamingo Award](../flamingo/overview.mdx)**, how much we actually know about
  them, and any award they hold from a **[voting record](../voting_records/overview.mdx)**.
* **Honest about who has agreed.** Being invited is not the same as agreeing to speak. Anyone invited
  who has not confirmed carries a **small star** on every poster and every list, and any poster that
  shows one carries the legend saying so.
* **No money moves through it.** No tickets, donations, sponsorships, fundraising or campaign
  coordination happen on a conference page. A conference where a slot can be bought belongs to the
  people buying the slots.

## Online first, in person later

The first conferences are **online**. The same record grows a location and dates the day there is a
venue. Before a conference, a party's site may also hold smaller online sessions along the way, such as
a conversation with one challenger or a panel on one [problem law](../problem_laws/overview.mdx).
