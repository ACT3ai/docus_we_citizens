# What the AI never decides

## It never sets an award

The Monkey and Llama awards are **published equations over recorded acts** — a weighted average you can
reproduce with a calculator. There is no model anywhere in that computation. Which acts exist is a
sourcing question; what they are worth is a published table.

## It never approves a record

Nothing about a living person is published because a model produced it. Every path to the public record
runs through review, and review is a human with a name and a stated reason.

## It never awards a point

Karma is earned by logged, evidenced, corroborated work and verified by people. The AI pass reads the
month and suggests; it does not score.

## It never decides what you should think

The product's job is to make your reasoning **visible and checkable**, including to you. It records
your positions, shows you where a chain is thin, and applies your values. It does not have a position.

## It never fills a gap with a guess

This is the boundary that matters most, because it is the one most tempting to cross. Where the record
is missing, the answer is *this is missing* — named, published, and offered as work somebody could do.
A plausible-sounding interpolation is worse than a gap, because a gap is honest and an interpolation is
indistinguishable from evidence.

## Why the boundary is drawn here

The movement's central complaint is that citizens are told to defer to an authority that will not show
its work. Replacing that authority with a model that will not show its work would be the same failure
wearing a different coat — and everybody would be right to say so.

So: **the AI reads, checks, and proposes. Humans decide, and the arithmetic is arithmetic.**
