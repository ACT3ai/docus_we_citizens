# Fix Bills and Laws

:::tip[The question this area answers]
**Do you have actual legislation?**
:::

Yes, and it starts from citizens rather than from a wish list. Libertarians add the laws they care about here — **including the ones they want repealed, which is its own kind of fix** — and this community decides which ones come first.

* **Generated from real problems.** A citizen names the problem; the legislative language gets built from that.
* **Prioritized by the community**, by the same voting and collaboration as everything else here.
* **A named bill beats a general statement.** There will be specific standing bills this community cares about more than the rest. Those get named and tracked, so a candidate can be measured against a bill instead of a slogan.
* **Partisan by design.** That is the point of a party door. The non-partisan .io does not host law work, so this is where it happens.

## Three different things get called "laws" here

* **Problem laws** — the enacted instruments that carry a problem, and the recorded votes that put them there.
* **Good laws** — repairs somebody has already filed: passed, defeated, or never allowed a vote. Each one records who blocked it. A bill that repeals or sunsets a problem law is the strongest kind there is.
* **Fix bills** — ours. Language a member could file tomorrow, that nobody has filed yet.

Keeping them apart matters: the most damaging thing you can do is mistake a law we document as harmful for a bill we propose. They are opposites, and one of them makes claims about how living legislators voted.
