# Captured Congress is the blocker

Both party bases have wanted the rigged layer gone, have voted for people who said they would remove
it, and have watched it survive anyway. The usual explanation is that the other side blocked it. The
record supports a duller one: **a captured Congress does not pass the fix that removes what captured
it** — no matter which side drafted the fix.

That single fact explains why two sincere agendas can both go nowhere for decades, and it is the
reason this area does not open with an economic program.

## What it looks like from either side

* A Republican fix aimed at the carve-out, the subsidy or the bailout arrives, gets thinned in
  committee, and emerges without the section that mattered.
* A Democrat fix aimed at the money in the process, the disclosure gap or the concentration arrives
  and takes the same route.
* Both bases then conclude the other side killed it. Frequently neither did: the provision was
  removed by people neither base can name, at a stage neither base was watching.

The gap between what was voted for and what happened is itself the evidence, and it is the same
signature the rest of this site tracks — see
**[A globalist deep state](../congress_for_citizens/the_globalist_deep_state.mdx)** and
**[Money in politics](../congress_for_citizens/money_in_politics.mdx)**.

## So the shared work is not one shared program

We are not asking a Democrat and a Republican to agree on which layer is the rigged one. They may
name different rules, different beneficiaries and a different order of importance, and each
community writes its own plan on its own door — see
**[One App, Four Doors](../four_doors/overview.mdx)**.

What they can do together is the step that comes before anybody's plan: **replace the captured
incumbents with challengers.** Not challengers who promise the right economics — challengers who are
strong enough not to be captured once they are in the seat, which is a different property and is
measured differently.

* **[Replacing Incumbents](../replace_incumbents/overview.mdx)** — the seat view, and rallying behind one challenger.
* **[Congress Working For Citizens](../congress_for_citizens/overview.mdx)** — what capture looks like in the record.
* **[Qualifications](../qualifications/overview.mdx)** — the published rubric a challenger is read against.
* **[Trust Scores](../trust_scores/overview.mdx)** — the arithmetic behind "strong enough not to be captured".

## Then each side's plan finally has somewhere to go

Once the seat is held by somebody who is not carrying the layer, the community's own package stops
being a wish list. It has to become **actual legislative language**: numbered sections, effective
dates, and the repeal list attached — published in the open, waiting for a sponsor, so a citizen can
point at the exact text and ask their representative why it has not been introduced.

* **[Fixing the Law](../fix_laws/overview.mdx)** — problem to bill.
* **[Fix Bills](../good_bills/overview.mdx)** — the bills themselves.
* **[Good Laws](../good_laws/overview.mdx)** — what passed, what was blocked, and by whom.

## The end state, in either vocabulary

Both bases describe the same destination in different words: the healthy economy left standing, the
rules the same for everybody, work worth doing, and nobody's advantage coming from having written the
rule. That is the ethics half of the argument rather than the economics half — see
**[Great American Ethics](../american_ethics/overview.mdx)**.
