# The fix both sides support

A repair that only one party's base will sign is not a repair. It is a plank, and it lasts exactly as
long as that party's majority — which is why the last several decades of them have not accumulated
into anything.

## Why a durable fix targets the mechanism

Because the rigging [damages both bases](/docs/non_partisan_site/what_crosses_both_parties/), the version that survives is written to be **signable by
both**. In practice that means it addresses the *mechanism* rather than the industry:

* The **carve-out**, not the company that benefits from it.
* The **disclosure gap**, not the list of people who used it.
* The **sunset that never sunsets**, not the program.
* The **standard whose compliance cost removes competitors**, not the competitor who survived it.

Two citizens who want opposite things from the economy can both sign a rule about who gets to see
what and who gets to decide. That is not a compromise between their positions — it is the layer
underneath both of them.

## The test

> **Would a Republican and a Democrat each sign this without having to pretend they agree about
> everything else?**

If the answer is no, it belongs on a party door, where a base builds its own economic package in its
own vocabulary. That is not a lesser destination — it is where most of the actual policy work lives.
See **[One App, Four Doors](../four_doors/overview.mdx)**.

## It has to become bill text

A principle is not a fix. What comes out of this area is **actual legislative language**: numbered
sections, effective dates, and the repeal list attached — published in the open, waiting for a
sponsor, so a citizen can point at the exact text and ask their representative why it has not been
introduced.

* **[Fixing the Law](../fix_laws/overview.mdx)** — problem to bill.
* **[Fix Bills](../good_bills/overview.mdx)** — the bills themselves.
* **[Good Laws](../good_laws/overview.mdx)** — what passed, what was blocked, and by whom.

## An economy that is ethical and works for workers

Both bases describe the same end state in different vocabulary: the rules are the same for everybody,
work is worth doing, and nobody's advantage came from having written the rule. That is the standard
this area is measured against, and it is the ethics half of the argument rather than the economics
half — see **[Great American Ethics](../american_ethics/overview.mdx)**.
