# Laws

:::tip[The question this area answers]
**What is the difference between a law and a bill here?**
:::

The movement keeps four separate law libraries. Which one a record lives in is decided by two
questions: **who wrote it**, and **did anybody let it vote**.

## The four

| Library | Who wrote it | Filed? | Enacted? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[Problem laws](../problem_laws/overview.mdx)** | Congress | yes | **yes — in force** |
| **[Good laws](../good_laws/overview.mdx)** | a legislator | **yes** | passed, voted down, or never allowed a vote |
| **[Fix bills](../good_bills/overview.mdx)** | **the movement** | **no** | no |
| **[Fixing the law](../fix_laws/overview.mdx)** | — | — | the *process*, not a library of records |

## Why the vocabulary is enforced

An **enacted law** is in force and governing people right now. A **bill** is language somebody wrote.
Conflating them is the single most damaging error available on this site — a movement that blurs *a law
that exists* and *a bill we would like to exist* has no standing to complain when anybody else does it.

So: a fix bill page never shows a vote count, because there was no vote. It never shows a sponsor,
because there is none. Its status language is about drafting, never about passage.

## The third fate

The category that does not exist anywhere else: **filed, and never allowed a vote.**

A bill can die in committee, be denied a rule, be left off the calendar, or be stripped in conference.
Each is functionally identical to defeat and none produces a roll call — so the record is blank, and a
blank gets read as *nothing happened*.

Here it is an event, with a name and a stage attached. That is what the good law library exists for.

## How they connect

```
a PROBLEM  ->  its ROOT CAUSES  ->  a FIX BILL closing some of them
                                 ->  a GOOD LAW somebody actually filed
a PROBLEM LAW  ->  the acts of the people who advanced it  ->  the Monkey Award
a GOOD LAW     ->  the acts of the people who advanced it  ->  the Llama Award
```
