# Law vs bill

## The words

**Enacted law.** Passed, signed, in force. It is governing people right now. Repealing it requires
another law.

**Bill.** Language. Somebody wrote it. It may have been filed and may not; it may have been voted on
and may not. It is not governing anybody.

**Filed.** A legislator formally introduced it. This is the moment a bill becomes part of the public
record — it gets a number and a history.

**Never allowed a vote.** Filed, and then stopped before any vote occurred. Not defeated. Not
withdrawn. Stopped, by somebody, at a nameable stage.

## Why the movement is strict about this

Because the alternative is the thing we complain about.

A movement whose pages let readers believe our drafted language is law, or that a filed bill was
enacted, is manufacturing exactly the kind of confusion it accuses others of manufacturing. And it
would be fair for anybody to point that out.

## The rendering rules that follow

**A fix bill never shows a vote count.** There was no vote.

**A fix bill never shows a sponsor.** There is none. If somebody files it one day, that is a different
record with a different status.

**A fix bill's status language is about drafting**, never about passage.

**A good law that was never voted on never renders as "defeated".** It renders as what it is, with the
stage it died at and who held it.

**An enacted problem law never renders as a proposal**, however much the movement dislikes it.

## The one that is easiest to get wrong

*"Never allowed a vote"* reads, to a casual eye, like *"voted down"*. It is not, and the difference is
the whole point of recording it.

Voted down means the body decided. Never allowed a vote means **one person decided for the body**, and
the absence of a roll call is the evidence of it rather than the absence of evidence.

## A note on identifiers

Machine identifiers, routes and stored field names sometimes lag the language — a published URL stays
routable forever, because breaking a link somebody shared is worse than an old word in a path. What
binds is every **rendered string** a citizen reads.
