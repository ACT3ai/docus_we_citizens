# How someone gets on the roster

## Step 1 — anybody recommends

Any user — signed in, signed out, admin or not — may **recommend** somebody. This was a promise the
product made in its own navigation spec before it was implemented, and it is now real.

A recommendation needs a name, a reason, and at least one source pointing at something the person
actually said or did.

## Step 2 — only an admin approves

Approval is gated. Nothing about a living person appears on the public roster because one person typed
it, and an unreviewed recommendation is never rendered publicly.

Trust affects **how quickly your recommendation is looked at**, never whether it is accepted. A
citizen who has done a lot of verified work sorts higher in the queue. Nothing is ever auto-approved,
at any trust level. Trust buys attention, never authority.

## Step 3 — the record starts mostly empty

An approved record joins the roster in the **unknown** Flamingo state with a low percent-known. That
is honest: we have a name, a reason, and a source, and not much else.

## Step 4 — the record fills in

From there it grows by contribution. Citizens add channels and appearances; transcripts are gathered
from material that already exists; assessments run against the published rubrics as enough material
accumulates. Adding a video or a channel is itself a logged, credited action.

## Step 5 — a legacy record, if they have one

If the person has previously held office, their voting record is a separate thing and stays separate.
They carry a voting-record award on one side and a Flamingo state on the other, rendered side by side,
neither one leaking into the other.
