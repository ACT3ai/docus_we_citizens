# Naming the gaps

Every missing link carries a reason. There are four, and the differences between them are the entire
point of this pillar.

## `classified`

The information exists, is held by the government, and has been formally withheld.

This is the strongest of the four, epistemically, and it is the least intuitive fact about it: a
`classified` gap tells you the material **exists** and **who has it**. That is information. It is a very
different situation from not knowing whether anything is there.

## `suppressed`

The information exists and has been actively kept from circulation — removed, unpublished, settled with
a non-disclosure term, or buried.

Differs from `classified` in who did it and under what authority. Often the harder one to source,
because suppression rarely announces itself.

## `blocked`

The information could be obtained, and someone is preventing the obtaining. Access refused, requests
stonewalled, litigation outrunning anyone's ability to fund it.

The distinctive feature is that this gap is **maintained continuously**. It is not a past act; it is a
present one, and it is happening while you read the chain.

## `unknown`

Nobody has looked, or nobody knows. **The honest default.**

## Why these must never collapse

The first three are **acts by somebody**. The fourth is a state of the world.

Collapsing them into a single "we do not have this" is exactly the move the deadlock depends on. It
converts *"the party this conclusion is about is withholding the decisive document"* into *"there is
some uncertainty here"* — which sounds like modesty and functions as a defence.

Keeping them separate lets a citizen say the true and specific thing: **the missing link is missing
because the subject of the claim is holding it**, which is itself evidence about the claim.

## What naming a gap does not do

It does not fill it. A named gap is still a gap, and the conclusion still has to survive its absence.

What it does is make the shape of your ignorance explicit, so a reader can evaluate your conclusion
against it instead of taking your word for how complete your picture is.

## Gaps are published, not hidden

The movement applies this to itself. Every calculation over the public record emits its gaps, and they
render on the page. A named gap is a real finding, and it tells somebody exactly what would close it.
