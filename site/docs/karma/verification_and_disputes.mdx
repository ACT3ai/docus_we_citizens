# Verification and disputes

Karma is only worth having if it is hard to fake. Most of the design here is about that.

## Every action carries its evidence

An action-completed record is one file with a **URL and a description**. The link is the point: a call
that happened, a post that exists, a meeting somebody else attended, a source that was added. An action
with no evidence is a claim, and claims do not score.

## Peer reporting

A citizen can report **another citizen's** work, positively. This is the highest-quality signal in the
system, because it is the one nobody can generate for themselves — and it is how the organizing work
that leaves the least paper trail gets credited at all.

## Verification, and freezing

An action is verified before it scores, and **its points are frozen at that moment**. That single rule
removes a whole class of gaming: there is no benefit to timing your submissions around a catalog
change.

## Anti-gaming

The rules that matter most:

* **Evidence a stranger can check**, not evidence only you can see.
* **Corroboration for anything that leaves no trace** — the organizing tracks lean on peer reporting for
  exactly this reason.
* **The month as the unit**, which flattens bursts and rewards records.
* **No self-corroboration**, in any arrangement.
* **A dispute lifecycle** with a real outcome, described below.

## Disputes

Anybody can dispute an action they believe did not happen or was miscounted. A dispute goes to an
adjudication queue and comes back with a decision and a **stated reason the parties can read**.

Nothing is silently reversed, and nothing is silently kept. The pattern is the same one that governs
proposals everywhere in this product: a refusal somebody can read is still somebody who was heard.

## What karma never buys

Karma feeds your merit weight in one arithmetic. It does not approve anything, publish anything, or
override an admin. **Merit buys attention, never authority** — and that rule is what makes it safe to
score citizens at all.
