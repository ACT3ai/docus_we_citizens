# Karma

<CTA />

**Karma** is the movement's record of the real political work a citizen actually did.

You log what you did — a call placed, a meeting held, a challenger vetted, a source added, an explainer
published — with a link and a description as evidence. Each action type is worth points. Points
accumulate. At the close of each month they are summarised into a **normalized monthly karma score**,
and from that point on everything downstream runs on monthly scores rather than raw points.

## Why karma is called a score when a politician's award is not

This looks like an inconsistency and it is a deliberate distinction.

**Karma is something a citizen earns** by their own logged, corroborated work, voluntarily, in a system
they chose to join. Scoring it is fair.

**An award is something the movement gives** a public figure on the strength of a public record they
never volunteered for. Calling that a "score" would make it a grade on a person, which is a claim the
movement does not make.

So citizen scoring keeps the word "score" throughout, and everything published about a politician is an
[award](../monkey/overview.mdx) or the absence of one.

## Raw points are treated as noise

A day's points mean almost nothing. Somebody with a free Saturday can outscore somebody who did the
hardest thing anybody did that month.

So the **month is the unit**. Raw daily points are working state; the monthly score is the record, and
it is normalized so it is comparable across citizens, across tracks, and across communities.

## Where it goes

* Your **karma dashboard** — this month, a per-week bar chart, and your lifetime total.
* The **leaderboard**.
* Your **merit weight** in the [meritocracy](../meritocracy/overview.mdx) — verified actions are one of
  its inputs.
* The **Leader History Book**, for contributions that changed something.
