# Inside a legacy record

Open any record and you are looking at four layers, stacked from most checkable to least.

## Layer 1 — identity

Name, seat, party, term. Boring, and the layer most likely to be wrong for people who changed
districts, changed parties, or share a name with somebody else. The frozen `key` — a slug like
`barbara_lee_ca` — is the identity the rest of the system uses, precisely so that a display-name fix
never orphans anything pointing at the record.

## Layer 2 — the acts

Every recorded act, classified into exactly one bucket. There are twelve, and they are the crossing of
three questions:

* Was it a **vote**, an **authorship**, or a **block**?
* Was it on a **problem** instrument or a **good** one?
* Did it **advance** that instrument or **resist** it?
* And did it happen **on the floor**, or in the small room where there is no roll call?

Each act carries its citation. This layer is the one a hostile reader should be able to audit line by
line, and it is built to survive that.

## Layer 3 — the awards

Two numbers derived from layer 2 by published arithmetic, each with a page that shows the derivation
term by term so a citizen can reproduce it with a calculator.

## Layer 4 — what we do not have

Coverage gaps, stated. A record with a thin decade says so rather than presenting a confident number
computed over a fifth of a career. Confidence is **reported, never scored** — it is disclosure, and it
never silently adjusts the award.

## The wall

Nothing outside the voting record touches layers 2 and 3. Interviews, background, education,
statements, and qualification assessments feed the [Flamingo Award](../flamingo/overview.mdx) instead,
and they never bubble up into Monkey or Llama. That separation is absolute and it is checked in the
build.
