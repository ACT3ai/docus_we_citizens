# Monetization and influencers

Every influencer is paid by someone, even if that someone is only the platform. That is normal. What
matters to a citizen is **who is paying, whether the payment is visible, and what it can steer.**

## Six ways the money arrives

| Source of money | Who can see it | What it can steer |
|---|---|---|
| **Platform payouts** — a share of ad revenue or engagement pay | The platform and the creator | Toward whatever gets a reaction. Party-blind, but it rewards heat over truth |
| **Subscribers and tips** — the audience pays directly | Usually visible as a feature of the account | Toward the audience. The closest thing to citizen-funded |
| **Brand sponsors** — a company pays for a mention | The audience, when the sponsor is disclosed on the post | Toward the sponsor's interest, in the open |
| **Campaigns and political committees** — paying a creator directly or through an agency | Often only in the payer's own spending reports, not on the post | Toward a candidate or a cause, often with no label the audience sees |
| **Foreign principals** — a foreign government or party, directly or through a firm | A public registration filing, when the law is followed | Toward another country's interests, inside an American audience |
| **Covert payers** — any of the above, arranged to leave no record | Nobody, by design | Anything. This is the case the whole area exists for |

The top three are ordinary economics. The bottom three are where a political message can reach a
citizen without the citizen ever knowing who bought it.

## Platform payouts are an incentive, not a payer

Revenue-sharing programs pay creators on the attention their posts earn. Nobody behind that money
wants any particular outcome, so it is not a hidden payer. It is still a thumb on the scale: an
income that rises with outrage pulls every creator, on every side, toward outrage. That is worth
knowing when a feed feels hotter than the country around you.

## Where the paid ones leave a record

Three public records already exist, and a citizen can search all of them without asking anyone's
permission:

* **Foreign agents.** The Foreign Agents Registration Act requires anyone doing political work in the
  United States on behalf of a foreign principal to register with the Department of Justice and to
  label the material. The filings are public in the FARA eFile database.
* **Campaign spending.** Federal campaigns and political committees report their disbursements to the
  Federal Election Commission, and those reports are public at fec.gov. A payment to a creator, or to
  the agency that hired one, can show up there even when the post itself carries no label.
* **Paid political ads.** The large platforms publish ad libraries that show who paid for a political
  advertisement. They cover ads. They do not cover a creator paid to post as themselves.

## The gap

The rules are uneven. A foreign principal must register. A campaign must report what it spent. But a
domestic interest group paying through an agency, for a post presented as personal opinion, can leave
no public trace at all. **The absence of a record is not proof of a payment**, and this site never
treats it as one. It is recorded as a gap, which is exactly how **[Evidence](../evidence/overview.mdx)**
handles every missing link: named, dated, and left open until the record fills it.

## Three questions a citizen can ask of any voice

1. **Who pays this person?** Look for a disclosure on the post, then check the three public records.
2. **Is the payment on the post itself?** A payment you have to dig for is a payment the audience was
   not meant to weigh.
3. **Did the message change when the money did?** Compare what the person said before and after a
   sponsor, a campaign, or a new platform deal. Their own recorded words are the evidence.

## Why the answer is counting, not silencing

Money can buy reach. It cannot buy a vote in a venue that only counts real citizens. A post seen a
million times still carries the weight of the one verified person who wrote it, and a thousand
purchased accounts carry none. That is the whole defence, and it works the same against a domestic
payer as a foreign one. See **[Trust Scores](../trust_scores/overview.mdx)** and
**[The mesh, and what it defends against](../trust_scores/the_mesh_and_what_it_defends_against.mdx)**.
