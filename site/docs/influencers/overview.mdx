# Influencers

<CTA />

For a growing share of citizens, political news now arrives through influencers rather than newspapers
or networks. This area follows the money behind that voice: who pays an influencer, what each kind of
payment can steer, and how a purchased audience is kept from passing as citizen opinion. It judges the
arrangement and its disclosure, never anyone's politics.

## The voice is not the problem. The hidden payer is.

An influencer who earns a living is not a scandal. Neither is a sponsor that is named on the post. The
problem is narrower and far more serious: **a paid political message presented as a citizen's own
opinion.** The audience believes it is hearing a fellow citizen, and it is hearing a client.

Citizens already suspect this is happening, and they are right to. When a talking point appears on
dozens of accounts in the same week, in the same words, the reasonable question is not *who agrees*
but *who paid*.

## Three kinds of money, three different problems

* **Platform payouts.** Paid on engagement. It steers toward whatever gets a reaction, which is heat
  rather than truth, but it steers the same way for every side.
* **Disclosed sponsors.** Visible on the post. The audience can see the interest and weigh it.
* **Undisclosed payers.** A campaign, a political committee, an agency, a lobby, a corporation, or a
  foreign service, paying for a message that carries no label. **This is the one this area is about.**

The full breakdown, with where each payment does and does not leave a public record, is on
**[Monetization and influencers](./monetization_influencers.mdx)**.

## The defence: count real citizens, not reach

The defence is not censorship, and this site proposes none. A movement whose whole pitch is *check the
record yourself* cannot also decide what you are allowed to read.

The defence is arithmetic. A post with a million views and a post with ten views each carry exactly the
weight of the one real citizen who wrote it. Purchased reach buys attention; it does not buy votes in a
venue that only counts verified real people. That mechanism is
**[Trust Scores](../trust_scores/overview.mdx)**, and why a paid crowd stops working against it is
**[The mesh, and what it defends against](../trust_scores/the_mesh_and_what_it_defends_against.mdx)**.

## How this area stays fair

* **The official record or nothing.** No living person is named as a paid voice unless a public record
  says so: a filing, a disbursement report, a court record, or the person's own disclosure. Anything
  short of that is described as alleged and attributed to its source.
* **The arrangement, not the opinion.** A hidden payer behind a message is the same problem whichever
  side the message helps.
* **A gap is recorded as a gap.** Where a payment should have left a record and did not, the missing
  record is noted as its own fact, the same way **[Evidence](../evidence/overview.mdx)** handles
  withheld links in any chain.
