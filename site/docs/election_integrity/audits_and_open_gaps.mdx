# Audits and open gaps

## Routine, not a concession

An audit that happens only when somebody complains is a **political event**: whoever demands it is
making an accusation, whoever grants it is admitting something, and the result is read as a verdict
on the complaint rather than on the election.

An audit that always happens is a **control**. Nobody had to accuse anyone, nothing is conceded by
running it, and the result carries information instead of implication.

The difference is entirely in the schedule. Same procedure, opposite meaning.

## Gaps are recorded, not argued away

Where a jurisdiction cannot produce what reproduction requires, that is written down as an **open
gap** — and it stays written down until it is closed.

* The gap names what is missing, who holds it, and what was said about it.
* It is not softened into "data limitations" and it is not inflated into an accusation.
* It closes when the material appears, and the closing is published too.

This is the same discipline used for every other kind of record on this site. A published gap is a
real finding; a confident guess is not. See **[Naming the gaps](../evidence/naming_the_gaps.mdx)** and
**[The public ledger of what is missing](../open_data/overview.mdx)**.

## Why the ledger matters more than any single audit

One audit tells you about one election. A standing, public list of which jurisdictions can and cannot
produce reproducible records tells you about the **system** — and it accumulates, which no individual
audit does. Ten years of that ledger is the most useful election-integrity artefact anybody could
build, and nobody has built it because it is boring and slow.

It also does something an accusation never does: it gives an administrator a specific, achievable
thing to fix, and credit for fixing it.
