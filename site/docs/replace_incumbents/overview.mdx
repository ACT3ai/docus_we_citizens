# Replacing Captured Incumbents

<CTA />

You get a captured incumbent out of office by beating them in an election. That is the entire
strategy, and the narrowness is the point.

## Elections, and nothing else

This movement replaces people at the ballot box. Not by pressure campaigns that end when the news
cycle does, not by hoping the right investigation lands, and not by anything outside the law. A seat
changes hands when more people vote for someone else.

Stating this plainly matters, because a movement that names capture and does not say how it intends
to remove it invites the reader to imagine something worse. There is nothing to imagine. **The
mechanism is a ballot.**

## Challengers first

Most political attention goes to the incumbent — what they did, what they said, what should be done
about them. That attention has a poor record of removing anyone. So the order is reversed:

* **Start with the seat**, not the scandal.
* **Find who could hold it** — including people who have never run and are not on anyone's list.
  See **[Good New Leaders](../new_politicians/overview.mdx)**.
* **Assess them before the primary**, while support can still be moved.
* **The incumbent's record is the measuring stick**, not the destination. See
  **[Voting Records](../voting_records/overview.mdx)**.

## Rally behind one challenger, not three

The reliable way an incumbent survives is a split field. Four challengers who broadly agree divide
the vote against a single well-funded name and the seat does not move.

So the hard part is not finding a challenger. It is **converging on one**, early, by a method the
community running it accepts as fair:

* **Merit decides, not access.** Candidates are assessed on public criteria, published with the
  reasoning. See **[Meritocracy](../meritocracy/overview.mdx)** and
  **[Qualifications](../qualifications/overview.mdx)**.
* **The community converging is the party base itself**, running the play inside its own primary,
  on its own door.
* **It has to happen before the ballot is set.** After the primary there is nothing left to
  coordinate.

## The same play inside each party

Every party base has seats it would like to fill better, and each runs this on its own door —
WeCitizensR.com, WeCitizensD.com, WeCitizensL.com, WeCitizensSocialism.com. No door tells another
door who to pick, and no door is written from another's playbook. See
**[One App, Four Doors](../four_doors/overview.mdx)**.

What every door shares is the arithmetic underneath and one rule about what gets shown: the seat view
always includes **the strongest challenger for this seat who is not from your side, whatever party
that turns out to be.** A tool that only ever showed your own team would be a campaign tool, and this
is not one.

## The seat view

The practical output is per-seat, because that is the unit a citizen actually votes in: who holds the
seat, what their record shows, who could take it, and how each of them assesses. The rosters and the
records live in **[Politicians](../politicians/overview.mdx)**,
**[Elected Politicians](../legacy_politicians/overview.mdx)** and
**[Good New Leaders](../new_politicians/overview.mdx)**.
