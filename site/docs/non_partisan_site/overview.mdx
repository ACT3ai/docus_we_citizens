# Why A Non-Partisan Site

:::tip[The question this area answers]
**Why is this site not taking a side?**
:::

<CTA />

Because the thing this site is for **is not a party disagreement**. It is the part that damages
Republicans and Democrats alike, that survives every change of control, and that neither party's
formal apparatus in Washington has fixed while holding the power to fix it.

There are five front doors. Four of them are party doors, and they are where partisan work belongs.
This one — **WeTheCitizens.io** — is the door for what crosses all of them.

## What only becomes visible when you stop arguing party

Run the two parties' complaints side by side and the overlap is the point:

* Both bases say their own representatives stop sounding like the district shortly after arriving.
* Both bases name money in politics, in almost the same words.
* Both bases have watched a position they voted for survive an election that was supposed to end it.
* Both bases have been told a file they asked for is unavailable, for reasons they are not allowed
  to evaluate.

Capture that damages both sides is not a partisan issue and cannot be fixed with a partisan
majority — because whichever side wins, the same forces are still there the next morning.

## A forum for deciding when DC will not

The second job of this door is a **cross-party citizen forum**: a place where the citizen bases of
both parties can reach a decision on a problem the formal party apparatus in Washington has failed to
fix.

* It is a **decision** venue, not a debate venue — positions are recorded, pooled, and counted. See
  **[Decisions](../decisions/overview.mdx)**.
* The unit of judgement is **a person and a vote**, never a party. No page here characterises a
  party or attributes motive to one.
* The output is meant to be usable by both sides at once: a named problem, a sourced record, and a
  fix that a Republican and a Democrat could each sign without pretending to agree about everything
  else.

## What is deliberately not here

This door is narrower than the party doors on purpose. The following live **only** on the four
`.com` doors, each written from that audience's own messaging:

* Partisan plans, and the partisan laws a community wants added.
* Voting on where you sit on the political spectrum.
* A board of directors, and the yearly strategic conference.
* Trust-score voting — the roll-up here is read only. See
  **[Trust Scores](../trust_scores/overview.mdx)**.
* Religious framing, where a door carries one.

If you want that work, go through your own door. **[One App, Four Doors](../four_doors/overview.mdx)**
explains which one, and why the record behind all five is the same record.
