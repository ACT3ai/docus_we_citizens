# What crosses both parties

Most political disagreement is real disagreement. Two citizens can look at the same country, want
different things, and both be arguing in good faith. This site has nothing to say about that, and
deliberately so.

This area is about the other category: the arrangement that **damages both bases at once**, that
neither party's apparatus in Washington has fixed while holding the power to fix it, and that is
therefore not a disagreement at all.

## How to tell the two apart

A cross-party capture has a signature, and all four marks have to be present:

1. **Both bases object**, in their own vocabulary, without having coordinated.
2. **Neither party's leadership treats it as urgent**, no matter which one is in power.
3. **It survives changes of control** that voters were told would end it.
4. **Somebody outside the electorate is better off** for its survival, and the record shows who.

A position where the two bases genuinely disagree fails mark 1. A live legislative fight fails mark
2. Something a new majority actually changed fails mark 3. If a claim fails any of them, it belongs
on a party door, not here.

## The overlap, in the bases' own words

Run the two sides' complaints next to each other and the shape is hard to miss:

* Both say their own representatives stop sounding like the district shortly after arriving.
* Both name money in politics, and both name *Citizens United* by name.
* Both have watched a policy they voted against outlive the election that was supposed to end it.
* Both have been told a file they asked for is unavailable, for reasons they are not allowed to
  evaluate.
* Neither wants their children in a war nobody chose, and neither has been able to end one.

None of that is a partisan position. It is the same complaint arriving from two directions, which is
the strongest available evidence that the problem is not either party.

## Why a partisan majority cannot fix it

Because whichever side wins, the same forces are there the next morning. An arrangement that outlasts
both parties is not going to be dislodged by handing one of them a larger majority — that has been
tried, repeatedly, in both directions, and it is the tried-and-failed status of that approach that
put this site here.
