# The qualification areas

## The list

**Competence in governing**

* **Constitutional governing competence** — does this person understand the machine they are asking to
  operate?
* **Governance and constitutional law** — the legal structure, and its limits on their own office.
* **Management** — running an organisation, which is most of what an executive office actually is.
* **Policy expertise** — depth in the subject matter their seat touches.

**Independence**

* **Anti-corruption** — the record on self-dealing, and on what they did when it was inconvenient.
* **Not controlled or influenced** — who has leverage over them, and what they have done about it.
* **Loyalty to the country** — where obligations lie when they conflict.

**Policy areas**

* **Economics** · **Domestic policy** · **Foreign policy** · **National security**

**The person**

* **Citizens' rights** — their posture toward the people they would govern.
* **Intellectual rigor** — whether they update, and how they handle being wrong.
* **Representation and leadership** — whether they can actually carry a constituency.
* **Public speaking** — the ability to make the argument in public.
* **Great life experience** — what they have actually done.

## Why this shape

The list is deliberately weighted toward **how somebody would govern** rather than **what they would
decide**. Roughly two thirds of the areas are about competence, independence, and character; the policy
areas are a minority.

That is the movement's bet: on most questions, citizens disagree legitimately and the right answer is
whatever a functioning process produces. What breaks a country is not the wrong tax rate — it is people
who cannot run the office, or who answer to somebody other than the public.

## The areas that are hardest to assess

**Not controlled or influenced** is the most important area and the one where the evidence is worst,
almost by definition. A rubric here has to be written against what somebody *has done* under pressure,
not against what can be proven about their relationships.

**Economics** is the area most likely to smuggle in a policy preference, and it is treated as the
worked example precisely for that reason — the mechanism-and-who-pays discipline was developed there
first and then applied outward.
