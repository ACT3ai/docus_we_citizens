# How a rubric works

## The structure

Each area breaks into subareas, and each subarea into **leaves** — the individual criteria actually
assessed. A typical area carries at least forty leaves; across sixteen areas there are 781.

Each leaf is scaled **1 to 10**, with the levels written out. Not adjectives — descriptions of what a
person at that level actually says and does.

## The mechanism test

The single rule that keeps a rubric usable across the political spectrum:

> **A high level requires naming a mechanism and saying who pays. It never requires reaching a
> particular conclusion.**

A candidate who says *"we should improve education"* scores low regardless of whether the assessor
agrees. A candidate who says *"here is the intervention, here is the evidence it moves the outcome,
here is what it costs, and here is who bears that cost"* scores high — whichever intervention they
named.

## What was removed, and why

Early drafts encoded positions as levels. *"More funding is the education 10."* *"Repealing this
statute is the permitting 10."* A pricing mechanism presented once as an obvious win and once as an
obvious harm.

All of it was stripped and replaced with mechanism-based criteria: outcomes measured against dollars,
statutory clocks, and the specific instruments involved.

The test applied afterward: **can a market Democrat, an industrial-policy Republican and a deregulatory
libertarian each reach a 9 here?** If not, the rubric is broken.

## Written before anybody is assessed

Rubrics are authored and published first. Only then is anybody read against them.

This ordering is not a courtesy — it is what makes the result a finding rather than a rationalisation.
A standard written after seeing the candidate is a standard shaped by the candidate.

## What the AI does with it

Reads long-form material — interviews, writing, public commitments — and marks it against the leaves.
Its value is **consistency**: the same rubric applied identically to everyone, including to people the
movement likes.

It does not decide whether the rubric is right. That is a human argument, held in public, against a
published file.

## Percent known

Every assessment carries how much of what we would want to know we actually have. A rubric applied to
thin material produces a thin result, and the number says so rather than letting confident-looking
output pass for a strong finding.
