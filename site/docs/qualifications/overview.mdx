# Qualifications

:::tip[The question this area answers]
**How do you judge without just judging by party?**
:::

A **qualification** is a named area of competence somebody running for office either has or does not,
with a written rubric describing what each level of it looks like.

Sixteen areas are authored, carrying **781 graded criteria** between them, each scaled 1 to 10 — and
all of it was published **before anybody was assessed against it**.

## The areas

anti-corruption · citizens' rights · constitutional governing competence · governance and
constitutional law · domestic policy · economics · foreign policy · national security · loyalty to the
country · not controlled or influenced · intellectual rigor · policy expertise · management ·
public speaking · representation and leadership · great life experience

Each is a file in the public record with a frozen key that is used verbatim as the filename of every
assessment written against it — rather than re-derived by each reader, which is how identifiers drift.

## The design goal

**A market Democrat, an industrial-policy Republican, and a deregulatory libertarian must all be able
to score a 9 in the same area.**

That is the bar the rubrics are written to. What earns a high level is naming a **mechanism** and
saying **who pays**. What loses points is having no mechanism.

When an early draft encoded a policy position as a level — *"more funding is the education 10"*,
*"repeal this statute is the permitting 10"* — those were pulled out and replaced with mechanism-based
criteria. A rubric only one political position can top is a broken rubric, and the movement wants to be
told when it has written one.

## The 0–10 scale

Rendered in eleven shades, with a companion **percent known** figure. An assessment that looks
confident but rests on one interview is a weak sample, not a strong finding, and the two are never
presented identically.
