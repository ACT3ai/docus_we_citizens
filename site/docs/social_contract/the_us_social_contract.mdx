# The US social contract

## What it is here

Two things, taken together:

**The Constitution** — the text, its amendments, and the structural commitments in it: enumerated
powers, separation of powers, due process, the franchise, and the purse.

**Representative democracy working as promised** — the operating implication of that text. You elect
somebody, they act for you, **you can see what they did**, and you can replace them. Each part is
implied by the structure; the third is the one that actually fails.

## What that lets us say

Concretely, these are the kinds of claim the standard supports:

* A duty existed and was not met, by a date, by a named body.
* A check that previously existed was removed.
* Public money moved without an attributable decision.
* A vote that the structure contemplates was never allowed to occur.
* Access was granted to parties who are accountable to no part of the public.

Every one of those is answerable from the public record and does not require agreeing with us about
anything.

## How it is stored

As **layers**, most general to most specific, mirroring the community hierarchy:

```
Earth  ->  Country  ->  State / Province  ->  Community  ->  Area  ->  Decision
```

Two parallel libraries use exactly this shape:

* **Facts** — the epistemology dimension: what we know.
* **Metaphysics** — its sibling: what is real, and what the reasoning is entitled to assume about
  causation, identity and truth.

Both are plain text. Both are assembled the same way: **start empty**, then concatenate only the
applicable layers top-down, each block preceded by a header naming its source file.

The result is a single assembled text handed to the reasoning step — and every line in it is traceable
to the file it came from.

## What the standard does not cover

Most of politics. Tax rates, spending priorities, foreign policy, immigration levels, and nearly every
question people argue about are **not** answerable from the social contract, and the movement does not
pretend otherwise.

Those are legitimate disagreements between citizens. The contract governs the *mechanism* by which such
disagreements get settled — not the answer.

## Other countries

The layer structure is why this generalises. Swap the country layer, and the machinery is unchanged.
