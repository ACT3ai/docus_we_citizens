# The wall

## The rule

**The in-office voting record feeds the Monkey and the Llama. Nothing else does. Ever.**

```
VOTES, AUTHORSHIP, BLOCKING    ->  Monkey Award, Llama Award
everything else                ->  Flamingo Award
```

"Everything else" means transcriptions of interviews, background research, resumes, education, public
statements, and qualification assessments. None of it touches the two voting-record awards, and the
voting record does not touch the Flamingo.

## Why the wall exists

Because the two kinds of evidence have completely different reliability, and mixing them would let the
weaker kind contaminate the stronger.

**A vote is a fact.** It happened, it is in the public record with a date and a citation, and the
subject cannot reasonably dispute that it occurred. That is the firmest ground the movement stands on,
and it is worth protecting absolutely.

**An assessment of somebody's interviews is a reading.** A careful one, against a rubric published in
advance, applied consistently — but a reading. It is legitimately arguable in a way a roll call is not.

Let the second into the first, and the Monkey Award stops being *"here is what this person did, here is
the citation"* and becomes *"here is what they did, plus our impression of them"*. The moment that
happens, the strongest claim the movement makes is only as defensible as its weakest input.

## Both can render at once

Somebody who held office and is now a new candidate carries both: an award percentage from the record
on one side, a flamingo state on the other, side by side, neither leaking into the other.

## How it is enforced

Not by remembering. The separation is a structural property of how the awards are computed — they read
from different sources — and it is checked in the build, in the same spirit as the movement's other
hard rules.

A rule that lives only in a specification is a rule somebody optimises away under deadline.

## The practical consequence for readers

When you see a Monkey or Llama percentage, you are looking at arithmetic over cited acts, and you can
reproduce it with a calculator.

When you see a Flamingo, you are looking at an assessment against a published rubric, with a
**percent-known** figure telling you how much of the material we actually have.

Those are different kinds of claim, and the product refuses to present them as the same one.
