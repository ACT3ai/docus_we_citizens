# Award, not a score

## The decision

**Everything published about a politician is an AWARD, or the absence of one** — usually a *percentage
of that award earned*. Never a score, a rating, a grade, points, or a verdict on the person.

In the terms of service and on every page, in the same words: **they were either given an award, or a
percentage of one, or they were not.**

## Why the word matters this much

Because of what each word claims.

A **score** is a measurement *of a person*. It implies a scale on which humans are ranked, and it
invites the reading that the movement has assessed somebody's worth.

An **award** is something *given*, for *specified acts*, by *stated criteria*, that the recipient can
dispute by disputing the acts. It makes the claim narrow and checkable, which is both more honest and
more defensible.

This is the one copy decision in the product with a legal edge on it, and it was made deliberately
rather than drifted into.

## What it changed, and what it did not

**Changed:** every rendered string. The gloss is *"Monkey Award (democracy damage)"*; the old
*"Democracy Damage Score"* appears nowhere. Page titles read **Awards for: name**. An uncomputed record
reads **No award yet**.

**Not changed:** a single digit of arithmetic. Not one weight, floor, or rounding rule moved. Anybody
reading the rename as a maths change is reading it wrong — and the movement's own regression tests
would have caught it, because not one asserted value moved.

**Also not changed:** machine identifiers, stored field names, and already-published URLs. A route
stays routable forever; breaking a shared link to somebody's award is worse than an old word in a path.

## How it renders

The **percentage big, the award word small** — a large `72%` over a small `Llama Award`. The number is
the finding; the animal is the category.

## What is deliberately not renamed

**Citizen scoring.** [Karma](../karma/overview.mdx), the citizen trust score, the leaderboard and the
monthly statement all keep the word "score".

Those are about citizens — something a person **earns** by their own logged, corroborated work, in a
system they volunteered for. That is not the same act as the movement **giving** a public figure a
number on the strength of a record they never volunteered for, and the vocabulary keeps the two apart.
