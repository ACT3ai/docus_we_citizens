# Meritocracy: Finding the Best Candidates

:::tip[The question this area answers]
**How do you decide who is 'best' without just picking favorites?**
:::

By track record, not by who we like. Meritocracy is the engine underneath everything here: find the candidates most dedicated to always work for citizens, and strongest at pushing off being co-opted.

* **Measured after the election, not promised before it.** What a candidate says on the trail is not evidence. What they did with a vote is.
* **Strong enough to say no.** Being good is not enough if the pressure turns you. We look for the ones the money and the machinery cannot move — and we show the record, so you can check it rather than take our word.
* **Matched to you, not ranked by us.** Republican voters are not one position, so we do not flatten them into one. You vote where you sit on the spectrum, and candidates get matched to that.

The same engine runs on all four doors. What changes here is the input: it runs on the political right's own priorities rather than on a neutral baseline. The arithmetic that turns it into a number is in [Trust Scores](/docs/r/trust_scores).

## What "best" is measured from

* **Two separate records, never blended into one.** What someone did with power — how they voted, what they authored, what they blocked — is judged on its own. What they said — interviews, background, public commitments — is judged separately. A speech never moves a voting-record result.
* **Most challengers have no votes to read**, so their own recorded words are the whole evidence base. That is deliberate: a newcomer is not penalized for never having held the seat.
* **The arithmetic is published, and you can redo it with a calculator** from what is on the page.
* **How much we know is published next to it.** "We know 31% about this person" sits beside the result, so you can see how much weight it deserves. Somebody can be fully known and still not good.
