# Join or start one

## Joining

Browse the communities on your install, find one working on something you care about, and ask for
access. World-read communities can be read before you join anything.

**What to do first, and it is not posting.** Read three or four existing decisions in the area you care
about — the chains, not just the conclusions. You will learn what standard of sourcing the group holds
itself to, which is the thing that actually determines whether your first contribution lands.

## Starting one

You need a name, a scope, and an access posture.

**Scope is the decision that matters.** The most common failure is starting a community around a topic
so broad that no two members are working on the same thing — at which point aggregation produces
nothing and the areas fill with unrelated decisions.

Better: start narrow, around a **problem** or an **investigation**, and add areas as the work actually
diverges.

## Stewarding one

**Set the sourcing standard early and hold it.** The first few decisions establish what everybody else
copies. A community whose early chains have unsourced links will have unsourced links forever.

**Name the gaps out loud.** An area with visible open questions attracts contributors. A wall of settled
conclusions does not — there is nothing for a newcomer to do.

**Do not resolve the delta.** When members reach different conclusions from the same material, that is
the most informative output the community produces. Chase it down to whether it is a missing link or a
values difference; do not smooth it away.

**Remember what stewardship is not.** Owners administer access and steward areas. They do not approve
anything into the movement's public record — that authority sits with admins, and no amount of standing
inside a community buys it.

## Individual Mode is not a lesser mode

If none of this appeals, ignore all of it. Individual Mode is complete on its own, it is the default,
and a citizen who never joins anything has the whole reasoning product.
