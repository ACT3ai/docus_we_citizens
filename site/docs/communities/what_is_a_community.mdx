# What is a community

## The model

A named workspace that many citizens can share, holding areas, which hold decisions. It has a frozen
key, a name, a description, and an access model.

## The access model

**Owners** — administer the community, manage access, and steward its areas.

**Writers** — record decisions and contribute.

**Readers** — read.

**World-read** — a flag. With it on, anybody can read the community without being a member at all.

Access is stated per community rather than inherited from a global setting, because the same citizen
reasonably wants different postures in different places: a public record for the work meant to be
challenged, a private group for work that is not ready.

## What a community is not

**Not a forum.** There is no thread. The artifacts are decisions, chains, and positions — things that
persist and can be cited.

**Not a jurisdiction.** A community is a workspace, not a geography. It may be organised around a
place, and it may equally be organised around a problem or an investigation.

**Not a source of authority.** A community position is the aggregate of its members' recorded
decisions. It does not approve anything in the movement's public record — that stays with admins, at
every scale.

## Weighting inside a community

Within the trusted layer, votes are weighted by earned merit, bounded at 2×. Where no merit exists yet,
weighting is flat and the edition records that it was.

Nothing about being in a community grants approval authority. **Merit buys attention, never authority**
holds at every level.

→ [Meritocracy](../meritocracy/overview.mdx)

## Storage

One directory per community in the state root, with a description file at its root and its areas
beneath it. Flat files. Keys are lowercase, underscored slugs, frozen at creation — so a link into a
community's area survives every later rename.
