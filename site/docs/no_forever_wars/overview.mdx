# No Forever Wars, No Draft

:::tip[The question this area answers]
**Will my kids be drafted into another war nobody chose?**
:::

<CTA />

This is one of the very few positions that comes out the same behind all five doors. Republicans,
Democrats, Libertarians and Democratic Socialists disagree about a great deal and agree about this,
which is itself worth noticing: **a position every base holds and no majority ever delivers is a
capture signal**, not a coincidence.

## Why forever wars survive every change of party

A war that outlives the administration that started it is being sustained by something other than an
election result. The pattern:

* The **authorisation** long outlives the thing it authorised, and no one is required to renew it.
* The **cost lands on people with no say** and the benefit lands on people with a great deal of say.
* **Ending it is made expensive to whoever ends it**, so no one does.
* Each incoming administration inherits the arrangement rather than the choice.

Voters have ended these wars at the ballot box repeatedly. They did not end. That gap between what
was voted for and what happened is the finding, and it is filed like every other one in
**[Problems](../problems/overview.mdx)**.

## No draft — removed, and kept removed

Conscription is the most extreme claim a government can make on a citizen: it takes the years and
possibly the life of a person who did not consent. The position here is simple — **no draft**, and no
reinstatement of one against citizen will.

"Kept removed" is the harder half. A prohibition that a future Congress can quietly reverse is not a
protection, so the goal is language with an actual lock on it. That work is
**[Fixing the Law](../fix_laws/overview.mdx)**.

## Who pays and who profits

* **Defense and research budgets sent abroad** — American money buying capability that ends up
  outside American control. See
  **[Foreign Intelligence](../foreign_intelligence/overview.mdx)**.
* **Contractors whose revenue depends on continuation**, which makes ending a war a business loss for
  someone with lobbying capacity.
* **Districts that pay in people** — and are usually not the districts that decide.

The way to see this clearly is one member at a time: what they voted for, and what it cost. That is
**[Voting Records](../voting_records/overview.mdx)** and the awards in
**[Politicians](../politicians/overview.mdx)**.
