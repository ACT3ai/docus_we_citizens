# Remove the draft

Conscription is the most extreme claim a government can make on a citizen. It takes the years, and
possibly the life, of a person who did not consent — for a decision made by people who will not bear
it.

The position is simple: **no draft**, and no reinstatement of one against citizen will.

## Registration is not a neutral formality

A standing registration system is the draft with the last step deferred. Keeping the machinery warm
means the decision to use it is a single administrative act rather than a public argument — and an
arrangement that can be switched on without a debate will eventually be switched on without one.

Any honest version of this position has to say what happens to the registry, not only to the draft.

## "Kept removed" is the harder half

A prohibition a future Congress can quietly reverse is not a protection. Most of the work here is
therefore about **durability**, which is a drafting problem:

* Language with an actual lock on it, not a policy statement.
* No delegation that lets the prohibition be undone by administrative action.
* A requirement that any reinstatement be voted on in the open, by name, in advance — so the record
  exists and the citizens affected can see who did it.

That is bill text, not a slogan. It is written in **[Fixing the Law](../fix_laws/overview.mdx)** and
published as actual legislative language in **[Fix Bills](../good_bills/overview.mdx)**.
