# What feeds it

## The inputs

* **Transcripts of their own words** — long-form interviews and appearances, at length.
* **Public writing** — books, articles, published positions.
* **Public commitments** — what they have said they would do, specific enough to check later.
* **Background** — work, service, things actually built or run.
* **Qualification assessments** — those transcripts read against the movement's published rubrics.

## Assessed per area, not as one lump

A Flamingo is not a single opinion. It is assembled from the **qualification areas** — economics,
anti-corruption, constitutional competence, foreign policy, citizens' rights, intellectual rigor,
loyalty, life experience and the rest — each assessed separately against a rubric that was published
before anybody was assessed against it.

That structure is what makes the award arguable. "This person is unimpressive" is not a claim anybody
can engage with. "This person scores low on mechanism in economics and high on constitutional
competence" is.

## The drill-in

Every rendered Flamingo is the top of a path that goes all the way down:

```
the award  ->  per-area results  ->  the criteria in that area
           ->  the material each conclusion rests on
           ->  a single video, and a single transcription of it
```

You can start at a number on a card and end at the sentence somebody actually said. That is the whole
design: no layer of the assessment is allowed to be a thing you have to take on faith.

## What never feeds it

The in-office voting record. Votes, authorships and blocks go to the
[Monkey](../monkey/overview.mdx) and the [Llama](../llama/overview.mdx), and the wall between the two
sides runs in both directions.
