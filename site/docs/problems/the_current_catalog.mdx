# The current catalog

## What is in it

The catalog covers a small number of structural failures, each with its own record and sources:

**Records withheld from the public.** Several distinct records covering matters of significant public
interest where material remains sealed, including assassination records still incomplete decades past
their statutory deadlines.

**Congress exempt from its own laws.** Rules applied to the country and not to the body that wrote
them.

**Settlements paid and hidden.** Public money used to settle claims, with the recipients and the
circumstances not attributable.

**No penalty for agency stonewalling.** The structural one underneath several others: an agency that
simply does not answer faces nothing, so delay is a complete defence.

**Foreign access to classified systems.** Access granted to parties accountable to no part of the
public.

**The economy rigged.** The largest and hardest record in the catalog, and the one most in need of
sharper root causes.

**Institutional capture.** Records covering bodies whose independence is the thing that made them worth
having.

## How to read an entry

Start at the **root causes**, not the statement. The statement tells you what the movement believes;
the causes tell you what could actually be done about it, and which parts nobody has closed yet.

Then check **the social-contract violation** — that is the link between "we do not like this" and "this
breaches the deal", and if it is weak the record is weak.

Then follow the **joins**: which fix bills claim to close which causes, and which decisions evidence
the problem.

## The gaps are the useful part

A root cause with no bill against it is the clearest possible statement of where a contribution would
matter. The catalog is not a list of complaints; read correctly it is a work queue.

## Adding one

Anyone can propose a problem. What makes a proposal strong:

* **A named harm with named victims**, not a general condition.
* **Separable root causes**, as many as you can distinguish.
* **The structural violation**, stated — which commitment is breached.
* **What fixed looks like**, concretely enough to check.
* **Sources.**

Approval creates a draft problem record, inheriting the duplicate guard and the frozen key. A decline
comes back with a stated reason you can read.
