# Write down your values

## Start with one rule

Not a philosophy. One conflict you have actually had an opinion about.

The format asks four things, and each one is doing work:

**`when`** — the conflict. When does this rule even apply? A rule with a vague `when` fires on
everything and decides nothing.

**`prefer`** — which side wins when it applies. This is the actual content.

**`unless`** — the exception that flips it. **This is the field that separates a rule from a slogan.**
Anybody can say they favour liberty. Saying precisely what would make them favour safety instead is a
real position, and most people have never written it down.

**`weight`** — how strongly you hold it, 0 to 1. Used for ties and aggregation.

**`rationale`** — why. For you, later, when you are wondering whether you still believe it.

## Order matters

Rules are read top-down: the first whose `when` matches and whose `unless` does not hold decides. So
the ordering is itself a statement about your priorities, and rearranging the list is a real edit.

## What makes a rule good

**It can lose.** A rule that never yields to anything is not a rule, it is an identity claim. If none of
your rules has an `unless`, you have written a manifesto.

**It is about a conflict you recognise.** Rules invented to sound principled do not survive contact with
a real case.

**It is narrow enough to be wrong.** *"Prefer freedom"* cannot be wrong and cannot be applied. *"Prefer
the less reversible option when both are defensible"* can be both.

## Principles and priorities

**Principles** are unconditional — *"Government derives its just powers from the consent of the
governed."* They are not this-versus-that and they do not fire on a `when`.

**Priorities** are the ranked fallback when a conflict arises that no rule covers. They are what the AI
falls back on rather than guessing.

## Where it lives

A flat file under your own user directory, keyed by your verified email or by the literal `default` on
a local install. Readable, editable, and yours. Versioned by you — bump the version when you revise,
and your old thinking is still there.

## The uncomfortable part

Writing this down will show you at least one place where two of your rules conflict and you had never
noticed. That is the feature.
