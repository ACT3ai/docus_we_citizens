# Policy packets

## The idea

A value system is a file. Files travel.

You can **publish** yours as a policy packet — into the movement's social network, where others can
read, evaluate, and adopt it. And you can **import** somebody else's whose judgment you trust: it is
cached locally under that author's name, and it is yours to edit from the moment it lands.

## Why this matters more than it looks

Most people do not have time to reason through most questions. The usual solution is to defer to
somebody — a party, a commentator, an institution — and that deference is **invisible, unexamined, and
total**.

A policy packet makes the same act **explicit and partial**. You can see exactly which rules you
adopted, from whom, and change any of them. Delegation stops being a habit and becomes a decision with
a record.

## What it is not

**Not a vote.** Importing somebody's packet does not give them your voice anywhere.

**Not permanent.** It is a copy. If the author revises theirs, yours does not silently change.

**Not authority.** A widely adopted packet carries no more weight in any calculation than one nobody
imported. Popularity is not merit, and merit is not authority.

## The identity boundary

**Publishing requires a real identity. Everything else does not.**

This is the charter's identity line, and it appears throughout the product for the same reason: reading
and reasoning are private acts that should never require you to be anybody, but putting something into
the public sphere under your name means having a name.

Note "real identity" here means a verified account, not legal-name disclosure. A pseudonymous citizen
can publish, and can reach the top of the trust ladder.

## Reading somebody else's packet well

**Read the `unless` fields first.** Anybody can list values. The exceptions show what they would
actually do under pressure.

**Read the priority order.** Where two of their rules would collide, the order is the answer.

**Look for a rule you disagree with.** If you cannot find one, either you have found somebody who
thinks exactly as you do, or you are not reading carefully.
