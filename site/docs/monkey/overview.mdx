# The Monkey Award

The **Monkey Award** is a single number, 0 to 100, published as a **percentage of that award**, that
answers one question and only one:

> **How much did this specific person do to put those specific laws in place?**

*"This voting record was given a 72% Monkey Award."* Higher is worse.

```
the MONKEY LIST      a curated set of enacted laws the movement has approved
  -> every ACT       each recorded thing this person did on each of those laws
    -> CLASSIFIED    into exactly one of twelve categories
      -> SCORED      each category carries a score 0-100 and a weight
        -> ONE NUMBER  the weighted average, published as a percentage of the award,
                       with a page you can reproduce using a calculator
```

## It is an award, not a score

Everything this product publishes about a politician is an award, or the absence of one. A person is
either given an award, a percentage of one, or nothing. It is not a score, not a rating, not a grade,
and not a verdict on them as a human being — and the language on every page holds that line.

## A 0 does not mean good

A 0% Monkey Award means: **we assessed this record against the list and found nothing on it.** It is
the absence of a finding, not a compliment. Credit for doing the right thing is a different award
entirely — the [Llama](../llama/overview.mdx) — and conflating the two is the most common misreading
available here.

## The wall

The Monkey Award is computed from the **in-office voting record alone**: votes, authorships, and
instruments blocked from advancing. Interviews, transcripts, background research, resumes, education
and qualification assessments **never** feed it. Those feed the
[Flamingo Award](../flamingo/overview.mdx). The separation is absolute, and it is enforced in the
build rather than remembered.

## No curve, no cohort

There is no bell curve, no banding, no floor, and no comparison against peers. Two people with
identical records get identical awards whether they served together or fifty years apart. What the
number measures is the record, not the neighbourhood.
