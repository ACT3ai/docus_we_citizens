# Who can join

## Two personas, and only two

Every surface in the product serves one of them.

**Citizen.** The customer. Can log in to join Community Mode, publish fixes, and be trusted by other
citizens — or can stay the anonymous **default user** and never log in at all. Reading and reasoning
locally never requires an identity. The principle written into the specs is *identify, don't gate*.

**Admin.** Configures an install and stewards communities. Login is required and the surface is behind
a role claim. Admins approve; nobody else does, at any level of standing.

## What you can do without an account

Nearly everything that is reading, and all of the reasoning.

On a public install, a logged-out visitor gets a **public read tier** covering the whole movement. The
write controls inside the pages are what carry the gate — and they **render normally**, they are never
greyed out. Clicking one takes you to sign up, remembers what you were doing, and returns you to it.
An invitation, not a wall.

On your own machine, the default user can use everything.

## Anonymity never caps the ladder

**A fully pseudonymous citizen can reach the top trust rung and full merit weight.** Merit here is about
work produced, not identity disclosed.

This is deliberate. Requiring people to expose themselves in order to be heard selects for those who
can afford the exposure — which is a filter no political movement should want, and a particularly bad
one for a movement about people with power over other people.

## What supporting costs, and what it buys

There is a paid supporter tier. It funds the work and unlocks a supporters area.

**It buys zero merit weight.** Stated as a rule, in those words, in the specification — so that nobody
can later introduce weighted influence for money as an obvious product improvement.

Merit is earned by information: verified work, corroboration, tenure, a published corpus anyone can
re-derive, and accuracy that held up over time. Never by money, invitation, or an admin's favour.

## What standing buys

Attention, never authority. A trusted citizen's proposal sorts higher in the review queue. It is never
auto-approved, and no merit score approves, publishes, or overrides anything.
