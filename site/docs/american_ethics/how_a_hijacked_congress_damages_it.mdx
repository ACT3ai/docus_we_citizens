# How a hijacked Congress damages it

Count a captured decision in dollars and you have counted the smallest part of it. The larger cost is
what it **teaches**.

The damage is not abstract, and it is not a matter of tone. It shows up as specific practices, each
recorded as a named problem with its sources attached.

## Exempting itself from its own laws

The body that writes a rule for the country carves itself out of it. Nothing about this requires
interpretation: the rule is published, the exemption is published, and the gap between them is a
matter of record.

What it teaches is precise and it is learned immediately — **the rule applies to the country and not
to the people who wrote it.** Every citizen who has ever been penalised under a rule Congress does
not live under has learned it personally.

## Settlements paid and hidden

A wrong is compensated with public money and the record of it is kept from the public that paid. Two
separate injuries: the conduct, and then the concealment of the conduct from the people funding the
settlement.

The concealment is the more damaging half, because it establishes that the accounting itself is
optional.

## No penalty for stonewalling

An agency declines to produce what it owes and nothing happens to anyone. Not a fine, not a finding,
not a career consequence. The obligation exists on paper and has no enforcement behind it, which
means it is not an obligation.

The long-running version of this is documented in
**[The withheld files](../deep_state/the_withheld_files.mdx)** — decades-durable, surviving every
change of administration, costing no one anything.

## Individually survivable, collectively decisive

Any one of these is small. A country can absorb an exemption, a hidden settlement, an unanswered
subpoena.

What it cannot absorb is the lesson all three teach together: **that the rules are for other
people.** That lesson is expensive, it compounds, and no appropriation reverses it.
