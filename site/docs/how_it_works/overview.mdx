# How It Works

:::tip[The question this area answers]
**What do I actually DO with it?**
:::

## The core loop

The charter describes the intended experience directly: a citizen is on social media, wants to go solve
a problem instead of arguing about it, and launches We The Citizens on their own computer. Then:

1. **Enter decisions and run calculations.** Record your position on an issue and let the platform run
   the comparison.
2. **Compare against what is ethically right.** The reference standard is the social contract — for a
   US citizen, the Constitution and representative democracy working as promised.
3. **Record instances of misalignment.** You, and people you trust, document specific places where what
   government is doing appears to violate that contract.
4. **Have AI double-check the logic.** Verified **from first principles** — not *an authority said so*,
   but *does this actually follow*.
5. **Confirm what is right and what needs fixing, then cast your fixes.** Commit to the decision and
   the proposed remedy.
6. **Publish, optionally.** A fix can be cast into the movement's social network as a structured post
   others can read, evaluate, and build on.

## What the app does for you

Assembles the context, runs the comparison, checks the reasoning, keeps the record, and shows you where
your chain is thin.

## What it never does for you

Decides. It has no position, it will not fill a gap with a plausible guess, and no AI output reaches
the public record without a human deciding it should.

## Individual and Community Mode

**Individual Mode is the default and is complete on its own** — local files, no server, nothing shared.
**Community Mode** adds pooling on top of the same app. You never have to trust a central server to use
the product.
