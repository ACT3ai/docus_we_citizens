# The Vote That Was Never Allowed

Most repairs to American self-government do not lose a vote. They never get one. This is the record of that: the fixes somebody actually filed, the gate each one died at, and — where the official record carries the claim — the person who held the power to schedule the vote and did not.

## What it does

The unit of storage is not a bill. It is a **campaign** — one directory for one subject, holding every round anybody has filed on it: the ones that passed, the ones voted down, and the ones killed in a back room, cross-linked to each other. Four attempts to close the same hole, stopped four times at the same gate, is an argument no single defeat can make.

The commonest repair is not a bill at all. When a harmful provision is already riding inside a must-pass vehicle, the only instrument that removes it before it becomes law is an **amendment to strike**, and it is filed with the House Rules Committee. It is either made in order, in which case the chamber votes on the provision by name, or it is not, in which case nothing is written down. An amendment submitted and never made in order appears in no roll call, no tally, and no member's voting record. On the public record it is indistinguishable from an amendment nobody ever wrote.

So the product goes and **establishes the negative**. When a search finds no standalone vote it retrieves the committee's own per-bill amendment list at `rules.house.gov` and records the resolution number, how many amendments were submitted, and for each strike-or-narrow amendment its number, sponsors, summary and status — submitted and not made in order, made in order, or withdrawn. The procedural votes that made the exclusion stick are recorded beside it.

Establishing that no vote exists carries the same evidentiary bar as a positive claim. Before the product will say a vote does not exist, the chamber's complete roll-call index is downloaded, its coverage verified, and the index registered with its address and checksum so the absence stays re-checkable. One run searched all 2,516 indexed Senate roll calls of three Congresses to establish that two named repairs had never reached that floor.

**`blocked_by`** is where a person enters this record, and the only place in the product where obstruction becomes a citable act. An entry requires three things, all present: a source URL to the official record, the **office that conferred the power** — because the claim is that this person held the authority and used it — and one factual sentence about what they did. Where a committee published a tally and no individual split, the row states that the person chaired the body whose gate the instrument did not pass, and makes no claim about how they voted. An unsourced blocker is recorded as a named gap and never rendered as a name on a page.

A sourced obstruction act is the heaviest act in the product: holding the power to schedule a floor vote and never scheduling one weighs above filing the repair in the first place. It raises that person's Monkey Award and nothing else. It never lowers anyone's Llama Award, because opposition here is displayed, never subtracted, and the two awards are printed side by side.

## The goal it serves

A politician who votes against a popular fix has taken a public position, and voters can answer it at an election. One who quietly ensures the vote never happens has done something worse: they have removed the record itself, and with it every other member's chance to be counted on the question. This is the single most effective way a law dies without anyone being recorded as opposing it, and it is why the [Good Laws](/docs/good_laws/overview) store exists rather than only a list of things that passed.

For a measurement system this is the hole that would have swallowed the whole exercise. A record built only out of roll calls under-counts the most powerful people in the building, because the more procedural power somebody holds, the less often they have to cast a vote to get their way. Read the votes alone and the chair who buried a repair looks cleaner than the backbencher who voted on it — a hole shaped exactly like the seats that matter most, and [what is documented](/docs/congress_for_citizens/expose_the_hijack) about how Washington works is largely made of it.

Writing it down changes what a citizen can do. [Passed, blocked, or buried](/docs/good_laws/passed_blocked_or_buried) turns a vague sense that nothing gets fixed into a dated, sourced list of who stopped which repair at which gate — the raw material for [replacing a captured incumbent](/docs/replace_incumbents/overview) in a seat where the record is otherwise silent.

## What keeps it honest

Naming the wrong person for killing a repair is the worst thing this record could do, so the discipline here is stricter than anywhere else in the product.

* **No source, no act.** "The bill died in that committee and the chair was X" is not evidence that X buried it. A published record showing X declined to schedule it, or a rule X's committee reported that excluded the amendment, is. Anything short of that is a gap, not a name.
* **Inference is forbidden, including obvious inference.** Where the record shows only that an amendment disappeared and not who moved to take it away, the stage is logged as withdrawn with no name attached.
* **The office is charged, not the room.** Only the officeholder who controlled the gate is recorded — which is how a member who had cosponsored the very amendment his own committee declined to make in order is never charged for the block.
* **We are not the record.** `congress.gov`, `govinfo.gov`, `rules.house.gov` and the states' own sites are. Every page here is one click from the primary source, or says why it cannot be.
