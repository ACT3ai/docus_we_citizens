# Show The Arithmetic

A number nobody can check is an opinion with a decimal point in it. So every award this movement
publishes about a named politician carries a page that shows how it was reached — each act, what it
counted for, and the division that produced the total. There are two such pages, because there are
two separate bodies of evidence, and they are never mixed.

## What it does

**The award invoice** is the derivation behind every award computed from a voting record. The word is
deliberate. It is an itemized bill: here is each line, here is what it cost, here is how the total
was reached.

It opens with a header band of three panels at the same size — the dominant award, the other award,
and who this person is. Neither award is shrunk relative to the other, because a page that quietly
makes one mark smaller has editorialized through layout. Then come the evidence tables, and the
harmful-law table is always first regardless of which award dominates, so a reader scanning down
always finds harm in the same place.

Each row is one instrument, and it carries:

* the bill and why it is on the movement's list, shown in full and never truncated;
* one generated sentence of what this person did to it, ordered worst act first, with repeated acts
  collapsed into counts and every act linking to the official record;
* that row's share of the two sums, written as a pair — `+263.0 / 5.0`;
* a short note saying where those two figures land in the equation.

Add the left half of every pair, divide by the right half, and you get the published percentage
exactly. That is the whole audit, and the table footer performs the division in front of the reader.

Under both tables sits the derivation. It writes the division out with the real numbers substituted
rather than in symbols — `2085.0 ÷ 23.0 = 90.65 → 91`. It lists every priced category this record did
**not** trigger, greyed and counted zero, with what each one would have cost — so the page reads as a
record rather than a charge sheet. It lists the rows that were excluded and why: out of office on the
date, wrong chamber, present, not voting, or dropped for having no source. A derivation that shows
only what counted against somebody is not auditable. It names the gaps that could not be closed. And a
**Disagree with this?** link opens a proposal against one specific row, carrying that row's identifier
with it.

**The Flamingo math page** is the other one, for a person assessed from their own recorded words.
Three tiles run across the top: the award with its mark, the "N% known" knowledge percentage at the
same size beside it, and the seat this person is put forward for. The award percentage is never shown
without that second tile, because 74 across three areas read and 74 across eleven are different
claims, and only the pair says which one is being made.

Two proportion strips follow — which qualification areas carried the number, and which of the three
authorities did: the AI reading, trusted citizens, and every other citizen. Then a per-area table
printing each area's own result, the share the movement committed to it in advance, the share it
actually carried, and what it put into the total. That last column visibly sums to the headline. Every
equation on the page lives in one band at the foot, rendered twice: once in symbols, and once with
this person's numbers put into the same steps.

Neither page sits behind a sign-in. Both carried a soft account prompt until it was removed. Published
arithmetic about a named living person may not have a login in front of it.

Both are re-derivable later. Every run is published as a dated, frozen **edition** storing the list as
it stood, the full policy object with both tables, and the run's gaps. Editions are append-only: a
correction opens a new one and publishes a changelog naming what moved and why — an act was added, an
act was re-read, an item joined or left the list, or the movement retuned its own tables and said so.

## The goal it serves

The claim this movement makes about a named living person is arithmetic over the public record, and
the entire defence of that claim is that a stranger can run it again. These pages are what make that
sentence true rather than a slogan. A citizen with a calculator, no account, and no reason to like us
can reproduce a published percentage from what is printed on the page.

That moves the argument to ground where it can actually be settled. Without the derivation, the only
available fight is whether the movement invented the number, and that fight is won by whoever is
louder. With the derivation, the fight is over a weight sitting in a public file — why blocking a
repair in committee is priced at thirty times a floor vote, for instance. That is an argument about
how power really works in a legislature, and it is the argument worth having. The reasoning behind
every weight is published beside it, which is why the answer to "your number is unfair" is
[do the math yourself](/docs/monkey/do_the_math_yourself) rather than a press release.

The reader is not assumed to be friendly, and should not be. Citizens have spent decades receiving
official figures with the working left off, from institutions that had every reason to round in their
own favour. A movement that asks for trust on the same terms has learned nothing. So the source is the
official record or nothing at all, and [the sourcing rule](/docs/open_data/the_sourcing_rule) is
published as a constraint we can be held to. What gets conferred is an
[award, not a verdict](/docs/politicians/award_not_a_score) — a claim about the movement's own
assessment, made with its working attached.

The frozen edition is the part that matters years out. A number published this month will be quoted
in a primary two elections from now, after the list has changed and the tables have been retuned. The
edition keeps that older number answerable on its own terms instead of leaving it as a screenshot
nobody can re-derive. Any citizen can pull the whole record and check it against ours, which is what
[Open Data](/docs/open_data/overview) exists for.

## What keeps it honest

* **The page performs no arithmetic of its own.** Every figure is read from the stored run. If the
  browser could compute a different total than the run did, the audit trail would be worthless. The
  one exception is labelled where it appears: the "what would this have been under a different rule"
  comparisons are worked out in your browser, and the copy beside them says so and says the movement
  did not confer that figure.
* **A derivation the data cannot support is never printed.** Awards published before the terms were
  recorded once rendered `0.0 ÷ 0.0` under a headline of 25, beneath an invitation to add the column
  up. Nothing divides to 25 from zeros. Those records now say they were published by an edition that
  did not record its terms, and drop the promise that the column adds up. The award itself still
  publishes: what is missing is the working, not the number.
* **Nobody is assessed on a vote they had no opportunity to cast.** Rows that fail the tenure test
  come out of both sides of the average — never a zero and never a penalty — and are listed with the
  service dates that failed.
* **An empty table still renders, and names which kind of empty it is.** "We looked and found
  nothing", "we have nothing to look against", and "we have not looked" are three different claims
  about a named living person. Collapsing them is the most damaging error available here.
* **What these pages never claim.** Not character, motive, or legality. Not a prediction. Not a
  verdict: a high figure is an input to an ordinary movement vote, never a substitute for one, and no
  figure triggers anything automatically. On the Flamingo page, "not earned" and "unknown" print the
  cause in words and never `0%`.
