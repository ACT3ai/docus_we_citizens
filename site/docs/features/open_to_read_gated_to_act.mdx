# Open To Read, Gated To Act

You do not need an account to read any of this. Every problem record, every law, every politician's page, and the arithmetic behind every award opens for a stranger who followed a link and may never come back. The one thing that stops and asks who you are is putting something into the movement's shared record with nobody's name on it.

## What it does

There are three access tiers. **Public** is anybody at all, signed in or not, and it covers reading the entire movement: problems, laws, politician records, evidence chains with their declared gaps, and the plan. **Citizen** is a signed-in person, and it covers writing. **Admin** is the install's console, and it is not rendered to anyone else — not greyed, not teased, not mentioned. A Citizen gate is an invitation; an Admin gate is a secret.

The published arithmetic is the sharpest case, and it carries no gate whatsoever. A politician's award pages — the derivation, the inputs, the line-by-line working — come from the public voting record and from people's own recorded words. An interstitial in front of that, even one a reader could dismiss, would be a block on showing it publicly.

What does stop is one act: **putting an unattributable contribution into the shared record**. A proposal, a vote, a video added to a politician's page, an action you logged. Who submitted a claim about a named living person is the one thing a later review cannot supply, so it is collected at the door.

The gate is drawn as an invitation. The control renders exactly as a member sees it — same label, same place, never greyed out, never a lock icon that explains nothing. Clicking it opens a short join sheet written for that surface: *"Sign up to put this problem on the movement's list"*. "Unauthorized" and "access denied" are banned from the product's copy.

Then it keeps your work. What you typed or selected becomes a **pending intent**, held in your own browser, never sent before you sign in, discarded after thirty minutes. On return the app lands on the exact page you were on, restores the draft, and reopens the control in the state it was in — and deliberately does not submit it. If more than one thing is missing, one reusable flow collects them: signing in first, one question per card, a way out on every card, and satisfied steps skipped rather than shown. There is no password to create and none to lose.

## The goal it serves

A sign-in wall at the front door makes a movement invisible to everyone who has not already joined it. Almost everyone who ever helps arrives as a stranger who followed a link, read one real record, and decided it was worth their evening.

Underneath the recruiting reason is a harder one. The entire argument here is checkable evidence about people who hold public power — [what they voted for and what they said](/docs/politicians/overview), sourced to the official record and published with the arithmetic attached. Evidence you must create an account to inspect is not checkable evidence — it is a claim asking for deference, which is the thing this project exists to stop doing. The [open data record](/docs/open_data/overview) can be cloned by anyone, and the app behaves the same way.

The moment a citizen reaches for a control is the moment they decided to take part, and an error message there loses the person. So that second buys an invitation, their draft kept, and a return to it — which is why [the things a citizen can do](/docs/take_action/overview) are shown to people who cannot do them yet. The one gate that does exist is the same commitment pointed the other way: attribution is what makes a claim about a named living person answerable, by that person or by anyone checking who said it.

## What keeps it honest

* **The browser is not trusted with the rule.** Every write from a caller with no account is refused by the server, with an invitation to sign in rather than an error. If a control is ever missed in the interface, the request still ends at the same join step rather than quietly recording something with no name on it.
* **The draft is never the write.** The pending intent lives on your machine and is untrusted by definition. It only ever reopens a control; the submission goes through the normal signed-in path.
* **No read route is gated, and there are no exceptions.** The four pillars — your positions, your value systems, your evidence chains, your own reasoning — stay open on your own machine, with no account.
* **Public means published, not everything on disk.** Another person's unpublished draft answers "not found" rather than "not allowed", because "not allowed" confirms it exists.
* **What signing in does not claim.** It proves an email address and nothing else. Who you are and which country's social contract binds you are asked later and recorded as your own statements — self-declarations, not anything checked against a document.
