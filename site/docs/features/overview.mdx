# Product Features

<CTA note="Every page below describes something the software actually does. Reading costs nothing and needs no account; an account is what lets you record where you stand and log what you did." />

The rest of this site makes an argument: that Congress too often works for
someone other than the citizens who elected it, and that the way to establish
that is to measure it from the official record rather than argue about it. This
area is the other half — the machinery that carries the argument out.

There is one page for each capability of the product. Each says what the thing
does in plain mechanism, names the political goal it exists to serve, and states
the limit it will not cross. If a page here cannot be traced back to something a
citizen can check, it does not belong.

## What The Movement Publishes About Politicians

The three awards a citizen sees beside any officeholder or challenger, and the two product-wide rules that govern what those marks are allowed to say.

<FeatureGrid>
  <FeatureCard title="The Monkey Award" to="/docs/features/monkey_award" goal="Make the accusation arithmetic a stranger can re-derive.">
    A published award, 0-100 and higher is worse, measuring how much one named person did to put a short, hand-curated list of democracy-damaging laws in place.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Llama Award" to="/docs/features/llama_award" goal="Find the allies, not just the opponents.">
    The counterpart award that counts repair rather than damage — 0-100, higher is better — for work that takes bad laws off the books or fixes the machinery of self-government.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Flamingo Award" to="/docs/features/flamingo_award" goal="Judge a challenger on evidence, and never publish silence as an accusation.">
    The award for everyone outside the voting record — read from a person's own recorded words and sourced background — and the only award with three states instead of a bare number.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Wall" to="/docs/features/the_wall" goal="Keep the heaviest claim answerable from government records alone.">
    A hard separation between two kinds of evidence: what a politician did with the power they held feeds two awards, and everything else feeds a third — and nothing crosses.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Awards, Not Scores" to="/docs/features/awards_not_scores" goal="Say something unflattering about a named living person, honestly.">
    The product-wide rule that everything published about a politician is an award, a percentage of one, or the stated absence of one — enforced by a lint that fails the build.
  </FeatureCard>
</FeatureGrid>

## Showing The Arithmetic

Every published number comes with the page that derives it, an honest account of how much evidence there was, and a dated re-runnable edition behind it.

<FeatureGrid>
  <FeatureCard title="Show The Arithmetic" to="/docs/features/show_the_arithmetic" goal="Move the argument to the weights, which sit in a public file.">
    Two different derivation pages — the voting-record invoice and the Flamingo math page — each built to be argued with, each reachable by anyone with no sign-in.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Say What We Could See" to="/docs/features/coverage_and_named_gaps" goal="Apply the movement's own honesty standard to itself.">
    Every calculation publishes its denominator and a typed cause for every blank — plus 'N% known' beside every award, and a public ledger of the data the movement is still missing.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Your State House, Same Instrument" to="/docs/features/state_legislature_coverage" goal="Watch the level of government that touches daily life most.">
    The same three awards and the same arithmetic reach state legislators, driven by a separate policy file — because the laws that touch a citizen most are often not federal.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Work You Can Watch" to="/docs/features/work_you_can_watch" goal="A number nobody can watch being made has to be taken on faith.">
    The recomputation is not a black box on a server somewhere — the job queue is visible, a run can be started and followed, and it keeps going if you close the tab.
  </FeatureCard>
</FeatureGrid>

## The Public Record It Rests On

Where the evidence comes from: every stage of every vote, the votes that were prevented from happening, the law pages, and the rule that a source is the official record or nothing.

<FeatureGrid>
  <FeatureCard title="The Whole Voting Record" to="/docs/features/the_voting_record" goal="Final passage is the last and least informative thing that happens to a bill.">
    Every recorded act a member took at every stage of a bill — committee, procedural, amendment, passage, conference, override — plus what they authored and what they signed on to, each row linked to the official record.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Vote That Was Never Allowed" to="/docs/features/the_blocked_fix_record" goal="Killing a fix quietly is more effective than voting against it.">
    The record of repairs somebody filed and of the people who used a position of power to make sure no vote ever happened — the single most effective way a law dies without anyone being recorded opposing it.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Law Page" to="/docs/features/the_law_page" goal="Every claim one click from the primary source.">
    One page per law: what it does, the rung-by-rung record of every stage where a question was put, and one click to the official source — plus the honest page for a law we hold no record of.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Official Record Or Nothing" to="/docs/features/official_record_sourcing" goal="Arithmetic over the public record is not defamation.">
    Every claim about a named living person carries a link to the government's own record, and an unsourced row is dropped and the drop reported rather than quietly kept.
  </FeatureCard>
</FeatureGrid>

## Problems, Laws And Fixes

The movement's second pillar: naming precisely what is broken, and writing the legislative language that would fix it.

<FeatureGrid>
  <FeatureCard title="The Problem Record" to="/docs/features/problem_record" goal="Grievance that stays vague cannot be fixed or checked.">
    A numbered, public list of exactly what is wrong with the country, each entry written precisely enough that a law could be drafted to fix it.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Fix Bills" to="/docs/features/fix_bills" goal="Close the gap between complaining and legislating.">
    Real legislative language the movement wrote for each problem — sections a legislator could file tomorrow — including a draft generated from the problem itself and from what citizens voted for.
  </FeatureCard>
</FeatureGrid>

## The People

Who holds power near you, who could replace them, and the evidence base the movement builds about people who have never cast a vote.

<FeatureGrid>
  <FeatureCard title="The Politician Record Page" to="/docs/features/politician_record_pages" goal="What your senator actually did, in the first screen.">
    The one page every link about a person lands on — in three shapes, depending on whether they hold power, are seeking it, or have never held office at all.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Rosters" to="/docs/features/the_rosters" goal="Open for any citizen to propose into, impossible to quietly write.">
    Four lists of people who hold or seek power — one frozen key each — that any citizen can propose into and only an admin can publish into.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Your Seats And The Seat Map" to="/docs/features/your_seats" goal="You cannot judge a vote until you know whose vote it was.">
    You say as much or as little as you like about where you vote — country, state, ZIP, street — and the app tells you live exactly which of your elected seats it can now show you.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Qualification Areas" to="/docs/features/qualification_catalog" goal="Publish the standard before the verdict.">
    The public, versioned list of named areas a politician is measured against — with the movement's declared weight for each one, and a slider so you can use your own.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Videos, Transcripts And Provenance" to="/docs/features/videos_and_transcripts" goal="A challenger has no votes, so their own words are the evidence.">
    The record of a person speaking in their own words — transcripts the app never downloaded itself, each carrying a visible grade for how trustworthy the transcription is.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Reading Their Own Words" to="/docs/features/reading_their_own_words" goal="Read hundreds of hours with no staff, and show the quote every time.">
    One dedicated assessor per qualification area reads a transcript against a rubric published in advance, quotes the words behind every judgement, and links each result back to the sentence and the timecode.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Who Should Run, And Where" to="/docs/features/contender_slate" goal="Answer the hard question — who should run, and where.">
    The run that turns a list of recommended people into a plan — one named challenger per seat, published with the arithmetic that put them there, and a reason beside every name that did not get one.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Worrisome Challengers" to="/docs/features/worrisome_challengers" goal="Hold our own side to the same standard as everyone else.">
    The roster where a citizen can file a sourced worry about somebody running for office — video-first, never anonymously, and published only after a human approves it.
  </FeatureCard>
</FeatureGrid>

## How A Citizen Takes Part

The working surfaces: reasoning through a question, casting votes, proposing records, doing real-world work, and finding your way around without an account.

<FeatureGrid>
  <FeatureCard title="Decisions, Areas And Communities" to="/docs/features/decisions_and_communities" goal="Reach a defensible conclusion when proof is withheld on purpose.">
    The workspace where a citizen works out one question for themselves — their position, their evidence, and what is missing and why — inside shared areas a group can own.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The First-Principles AI Check" to="/docs/features/first_principles_ai_check" goal="Check the logic, never supply a new unaccountable authority.">
    Ask the app to re-check a decision's reasoning using only the background material you gave it, and get back a step-by-step chain you can audit line by line.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Value Systems And Policy Packets" to="/docs/features/value_systems_and_policy_packets" goal="The answer to being told what to think.">
    Write down your own this-versus-that rules as structured ethics the software can apply, then publish them for others to import — or import somebody else's.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Citizen Votes" to="/docs/features/citizen_votes" goal="Answerable by the people the movement claims to speak for.">
    Stars, yes-or-no ballots and plan answers — one voting system pointed at problems, laws, people and the movement itself, sealed daily into a number anyone can recompute.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Movement Plan" to="/docs/features/the_movement_plan" goal="Tell a citizen who just decided to help what happens next.">
    The movement's written statement of what it is going to do, published as a numbered document a citizen reads end to end — with a vote button inside every item.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Proposals And Review" to="/docs/features/proposals_and_review" goal="A movement that drops citizen contributions stops receiving them.">
    The one place a citizen who is not an admin can change what the movement says — and the review side, which on your own laptop is you.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Actions You Can Actually Take" to="/docs/features/activism_catalog" goal="Say what to do on Saturday morning, not just what is wrong.">
    A wall of action cards, each opening a step-by-step walkthrough that ends in an 'I did it' button and a form for logging what you did, with evidence a stranger can check.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Karma And The History Book" to="/docs/features/karma_and_leaderboard" goal="Make the movement's scarce resource countable.">
    Recognition a citizen earns for real-world work that other citizens verified, shown on public boards titled the History Book of Citizens Fixing Democracy — and itemised line by line.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Search The Record" to="/docs/features/search_the_record" goal="Evidence nobody can find is worthless.">
    One search box in the header of every page finds a politician or a law by name and takes you straight to its page — ungated, and logging nothing.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The AI Assistant" to="/docs/features/ai_assistant" goal="Answer the question without ever supplying the opinion.">
    A chat box beside every record that answers questions about what is on the screen, cites what it read, and refuses to hold a political opinion of its own.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Open To Read, Gated To Act" to="/docs/features/open_to_read_gated_to_act" goal="A sign-in wall makes the argument invisible to everyone outside it.">
    A stranger reads the entire movement with no account, every control they cannot use yet is still on screen, and clicking one keeps their draft and hands it back afterwards.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Your Settings, Your Data" to="/docs/features/your_settings_and_data" goal="Leaving costs nothing, so this never becomes something you are inside.">
    The controls a citizen actually holds: what the AI is allowed to do, what gets published, a full export of everything at any time, and closing the account.
  </FeatureCard>
</FeatureGrid>

## Trust Between Citizens

How the movement weighs whose input counts without letting anyone buy standing, and without ever requiring a real name.

<FeatureGrid>
  <FeatureCard title="The Trust Ladder" to="/docs/features/trust_ladder" goal="Survive astroturf without putting a login wall at the door.">
    The rungs a citizen climbs by doing verifiable work, which decide what their input can do — climbable under a pseudonym, and impossible to buy.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Where You Stand" to="/docs/features/trust_wizard" goal="Where you actually stand, not how you describe yourself.">
    A skippable, resumable walk that shows you real people two at a time and one at a time, and works out where you actually stand from your answers rather than from a label.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Groups And Values Blocks" to="/docs/features/groups_and_values_blocks" goal="Tell a real public from manufactured accounts.">
    Named communities built around written positions, where your claim to belong is checked against your own answers and — if you volunteer it — your own public record.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Your Profile, Your Anonymity" to="/docs/features/citizen_profiles_and_anonymity" goal="Pseudonymity as a safety feature, not a convenience toggle.">
    Take part under a pseudonym, your real name, or both — with a one-click switch that removes you from public view without costing you your standing or your work.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Verification Layers" to="/docs/features/verification_layers" goal="Prove control of something without proving who you are.">
    A dashboard of independent layers a citizen may choose to prove — each one proving control of something and nothing more, and none of them a gate on reading or taking part.
  </FeatureCard>
</FeatureGrid>

## Yours To Run, Yours To Check

The software is a tool a citizen holds, the evidence is a public repository anyone can clone, and the rules the movement promises to follow are enforced by things that fail loudly.

<FeatureGrid>
  <FeatureCard title="Run It On Your Own Computer" to="/docs/features/run_on_your_own_computer" goal="A tool you own cannot be switched off or quietly changed.">
    The whole product is a program you run on your own machine with no account, no database and no network — and the hosted copy is the same code with one driver swapped.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Public Data Repository" to="/docs/features/public_data_repository" goal="Do not ask for the deference the movement exists to refuse.">
    Everything the movement asserts about laws, votes and politicians lives in a separate public git repository anyone can clone and diff — and a file being in it is the approval record.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Citizen Mining Network" to="/docs/features/citizen_mining_network" goal="An impossible bill for one server, an afternoon for thirty citizens.">
    The movement publishes the list of videos it has no transcript for, and volunteers run the work on their own computers into public repositories they own — with independent copies cross-checked before anything is quoted.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Nothing Fails Quietly" to="/docs/features/nothing_fails_quietly" goal="A named gap beats a quiet one — turned inward on the software.">
    Loading, empty and broken are never confused for one another on any page — and the rules the movement promises to follow are enforced by build-failing checks rather than by anyone remembering.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Paid Supporter" to="/docs/features/paid_supporter" goal="Pay for it without advertising, paywalls, or selling citizens out.">
    A voluntary monthly membership at an amount you choose, buying exactly one thing — a room showing where the money went — and never a louder vote.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="The Four Party Front Doors" to="/docs/features/four_party_front_doors" goal="In-party persuasion is fast; cross-party persuasion is not.">
    Four additional websites — for Republicans, Democrats, Libertarians and Democratic Socialists — running the same software and publishing the same numbers as this one.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Publishing To The Social Network" to="/docs/features/publishing_to_the_social_network" goal="Exchange the reasoning, not the conclusion.">
    A finished piece of reasoning can be cast outward as a structured post that other citizens can evaluate and build on, rather than an opinion shouted into a feed.
  </FeatureCard>
</FeatureGrid>
