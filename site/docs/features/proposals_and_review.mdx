# Proposals And Review

Almost everything this movement publishes it took from the official record. This is the other door: the form a citizen fills in to add something the record does not carry yet, or to say that an existing record is wrong. On the far side of it is the console where a submission gets read before anything is published. It exists because a movement that quietly drops what citizens send it stops being sent anything.

## What it does

Six kinds of submission share one form, one inbox and one queue. A **problem** the movement has not listed. The **enacted law** that causes one of the problems already listed. A **good law** that would have repaired something, together with who stopped it. A **fix bill** — legislative language that would undo the damage. A **person** who belongs on one of the rosters. And **feedback** saying an existing record is wrong, incomplete, or missing a source.

Every one of them requires a written **rationale**. A submission with no argument in it is not a proposal and the form will not send it.

Then the door does the harder work. A claim about a named living person, an enacted law, or a blocker is **refused outright at submission** without a source URL, because a citation demanded only at the end leaves uncited claims about living people sitting in an inbox in the meantime. The strictest version is the "who stopped it" claim: asserting that somebody used a position of power to prevent a vote requires the official record, the office held, and one factual sentence about what the person did. "The bill died in that committee and they were the chair" is refused as an inference, and the refusal says that is the reason.

Most kinds are **promoted in the same request that files them**. The waiting was removed; the machinery was not. Filing runs the identical path an approval runs — the same key minting and freezing, the same duplicate guards, the same audit row — and the record states plainly that it was approved automatically. The deciding party is recorded as `auto` and never as a person's name, because a citizen reading their own approved submission must not be told a human looked at it.

One thing still waits for a human: putting a **named person onto a movement roster** in a public repository. Every other kind describes a thing. That one describes somebody the movement is willing to stand behind, and its blast radius is a person's standing rather than a record's accuracy.

The review side opens the whole submission with nothing truncated: sources as clickable links with their hosts shown, videos playable in place, unrecognised fields displayed rather than dropped. Above the decision buttons sits a standing checklist. **Approve** is hard-disabled where a required piece is missing, with the reason printed on the button itself. There is **no bulk approve at any count**. **Decline** requires a reason, and the author is shown it word for word.

Nothing is hard-deleted. A declined submission stays readable by the person who sent it, because what the movement was asked and chose not to do is part of its record. On a citizen's own machine the person holding all of this is the person at the keyboard: whoever is physically on the box owns the box, and the review console is theirs.

## The goal it serves

A movement that silently drops citizen contributions stops receiving them. That is not a customer-service problem, it is a truth-gathering problem. The people who know that a law is doing damage, or that a challenger's record has been misdescribed, are not sitting in a research office. They will send it once. If nothing visibly happens, they will not send it twice, and the movement's record stays as thin as whoever staffs it.

The same reasoning runs the other way. A movement that refuses information because of who it came from is not seeking truth. A politician correcting the record of their own votes is exactly the correction we want, so their submission is **labelled**, permanently and visibly, and never blocked. We do not refuse information based on its source; we refuse to hide where it came from. [Open Data](/docs/open_data/overview) is the finished version of that promise, and [proposing a correction](/docs/open_data/propose_a_correction) is the same act aimed at a record that already exists.

The second half of the goal is aimed at us rather than at Washington. Everything published here about a person is checkable arithmetic over the official record, and that only holds if nothing negative about a named living person reaches the public record because somebody skimmed one truncated line and clicked through. The console is built to make that specific failure hard: read the whole thing, or you cannot approve it. The [catalog of citizen actions](/docs/take_action/overview) treats a filed proposal as real work, and the [problem records](/docs/problems/overview) and [problem laws](/docs/problem_laws/overview) most submissions land in are why the standard has to hold.

## What keeps it honest

* **The gate is at the door, not at the decision.** No rationale, no submission. No citation on a claim about a person or an enacted law, no submission. Removing the wait did not move that bar.
* **Approve refuses out loud.** Zero sources on a kind that requires them, a record that cannot be read, a submission already decided — each disables the button and prints why on it. A silently greyed control is not a guard, it is a mystery.
* **No bulk approve exists at any count.** A queue of a hundred good-faith submissions is a good problem. A hundred unread approvals about named living people is an incident.
* **A refusal is answered in writing.** Decline and ask-for-changes both require a reason, and the author reads it verbatim on their own page.
* **Deletion is narrow and leaves a tombstone.** A submission carrying a private person's home address, or an unsourced accusation, can be destroyed — with a required reason, an audit row naming the id, the author, the admin, the date and a hash of what was removed, and no bulk version at any count. The content goes; the accountability does not. An author's own equivalent is to withdraw, which keeps the record and marks it.
* **What is not built, plainly.** In community mode a proposal should be able to gather co-signers before anyone reviews it, so the queue reflects support and not only age. That does not exist yet.
