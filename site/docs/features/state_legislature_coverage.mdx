# Your State House, Same Instrument

Your state legislature passes the laws you meet first: what a landlord may do, what a ballot has to prove, what a small business has to file, what your county sheriff is funded to do. It is also the level of American government almost nobody watches. This is the same instrument the movement uses on Congress, aimed at state chambers instead — the same act categories, the same equation, its own policy file, and its own refusal to guess who cast a vote.

## What it does

This is a question of **scope, not a fourth award**. The same classifier reads a state act as reads a federal one, into the same twelve categories, through the same weighted average with the same floors. What changes is which corpus is open and which table prices the acts. State acts are priced from a **separate state policy file** whose forty-eight values ship as exact copies of the federal ones, and a build check compares the two tables and prints any difference — so a deliberate departure is loud and an accidental one is visible.

Getting the record is three jobs, and only one may ever be cited. A **finder** answers whether a roll call exists. A **locator** says where the state itself published it. A **citation** is the state's own bytes, fetched by us, and it is the only thing a committed row may point at. An aggregator that mislabels a motion costs one wasted fetch; a row citing a page nobody opened is a public claim about a living person resting on somebody else's transcription. All fifty states have a reachable vote-bearing host. Transport is nearly solved. Identity is not, and that is the actual work.

**The state wall** is the containment rule this level needed and the federal one never did: a member is charged only for instruments of their own state, only for roll calls of a chamber they sat in, and only for a session they served in. Fifty corpora share one directory tree and one roster, and a leak between them does not look like a bug — it looks like a Minnesota representative with a California record. A build guard fails the build rather than trusting a filter somebody can forget.

Then the surname problem. State journals print surnames, and no state legislator has a federal identity number — there is no such thing and there never will be. Where the printed form matches two sitting members, the row **resolves to nobody**: written to a separate file, counted into the totals and into the coverage denominator, charged to no one, and said out loud on the law page — *2 of 134 recorded positions could not be resolved to a named member from the journal alone.*

Two pieces make it yours rather than abstract:

* **The seat.** A ZIP-to-district crosswalk built from the Census Bureau's relationship file for current lower-chamber districts. A ZIP split across two districts resolves nothing and asks you which one you vote in.
* **Multi-member districts.** Washington elects two representatives per district to numbered ballot positions. Which position a member holds comes from the Secretary of State's certified results or from nowhere: an unsourced position fills no seat, and that seat shows its no-record state. Assigning one by alphabet or seniority would be a confident wrong answer with no error attached.

Out of it comes a Monkey Award and a Llama Award over that person's state acts, with the act count beside them. Someone who served at both levels also gets a career pair: one weighted average over the union of their acts, each priced by the table of the level it happened at, published as three rows — state, federal, career — and never as the mean of two published awards. Transcripts, interviews and public commitments never touch any of this; they feed the Flamingo Award only.

## The goal it serves

Accountability that stops at Congress stops above the laws most people actually live under. The movement already held identity records for more than seven thousand sitting state legislators, and every one carried a line saying there was no mechanism to read their votes. That was true, and it was a hole rather than a boundary: those people cast recorded votes, on instruments already filed as problems, and the product built to measure what politicians did was measuring none of it.

It is also where replacement is cheapest. A captured incumbent is removed at an election, and state races are the ones a citizen can reach — closer, smaller, decided by fewer votes than any federal seat. The evidence is the same kind the federal side runs on: a dated act of official power, cited to the chamber's own journal. See [Voting Records](/docs/voting_records/overview) for how that record is read, [Elected Politicians](/docs/legacy_politicians/overview) for what one looks like, and [Replacing Captured Incumbents](/docs/replace_incumbents/overview) for what the movement does with it.

The refusal matters as much as the coverage. Measuring below Congress multiplies the chances to say something perfectly true about the wrong person, because a state journal hands you a surname where Congress hands you an identifier. The [Monkey Award](/docs/monkey/overview) and the [Llama Award](/docs/llama/overview) are worth something only if the arithmetic is checkable and the person under it is the right one — which is why [the wall](/docs/politicians/the_wall) and the sourcing rule come down to the state level unchanged, rather than being relaxed to get more states online faster.

## What keeps it honest

* **Turning a state on is an approval, not a side effect.** A coverage file records who decided it, on what date, and why in a written sentence. Today one state is on. Two more have complete rosters and no instrument corpus behind them, and their rows say exactly that.
* **Forty-nine states report "not covered," not "not yet assessed."** Roughly seven thousand records in uncovered states carry a typed cause and a sourcing sentence. None of them renders as a zero, because a zero would mean we looked.
* **A thin record is published with its denominator.** Fewer than three acts marks an award low-confidence, the act count leads the page rather than hiding in a tooltip, and the number is still published — withholding a computed number out of caution is treated as a defect, not as care.
* **A denominator we cannot compute is printed as unknown.** Where a member's terms of service are not sourced, the page says *unknown (tenure not sourced)* instead of quietly substituting the count of acts we happened to find.
* **No model output reaches a state award.** Not about a person and not about an instrument. Every number traces to a roll call in a public file, cited to the chamber's own journal, and to decisions a named administrator made in the open.
* **Undecided is stated, not skipped.** In the one covered state, eight roll calls are declared and two have been decided as counting. The other six are recorded as undecided and named — a work item, not a silence.
