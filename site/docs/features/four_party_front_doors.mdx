# The Four Party Front Doors

Besides this site, We The Citizens runs four more: WeCitizensR.com for Republicans, WeCitizensD.com for Democrats, WeCitizensL.com for Libertarians, and WeCitizensSocialism.com for Democratic Socialists. They are not four organizations and not four data sets. They are one running program with one public record behind it, rendered four ways, because a voter will look far harder at their own side's officeholders than at a site with no label they recognize.

## What it does

Four party domains are served by one running web app with one data store behind it. The **edition** — R, D, L or S — is worked out from the address the request arrived on, and nothing further down reads the raw hostname; every page asks for the resolved edition instead. An address the app does not recognize falls back to the neutral main edition rather than dropping a stranger onto somebody else's party page.

Each door opens on its own front page, carries its own accent colour, its own wording, and its own set of content areas, written from that audience's own messaging. The four are **independent, not mirrors**. One door may carry areas another does not, and improving one creates no obligation to change the rest. What every door does carry is a link to the other three, in the header and in the footer, so no door pretends the others are not there.

What a door is allowed to change is chrome and wording, and that is the whole list. The award arithmetic, the thresholds, the weights, who is on a roster, the evidence and the sources are computed once and party-blind. The same number means the same thing on every door, and an incumbent renders exactly as harshly on their own party's door as on anyone else's.

That is held in place structurally rather than by good intentions. The edition configuration file has nowhere in its shape to put a threshold, and a build check fails on any committed difference between the doors outside the small permitted set: domain, display name, party words, accent, analytics tag. A party colour may tint a chip, a link and one button. It may never tint an award bar, where red means bad and green means good on all four doors.

The main surface on each door is the **seat panel**: for each seat you actually vote on, the officeholder, the strongest challenger from your own side, and the strongest challenger who is not from your side, whoever that turns out to be. Third column, same photograph size, same arithmetic. When there is no such person yet, the honest empty state renders in the full width of the column instead of the column being quietly dropped.

## The goal it serves

A committed partisan who lands on a site with no label reads it as *this is for the other team*, and stops reading. That reaction is not stupidity. It is a reasonable defence against being argued at, and it is the reason the neutral front door alone was never going to be enough.

Cross-party persuasion is expensive and slow. In-party accountability is cheap and fast, because the visitor already agrees with the label on the door and only needs to be shown what this particular incumbent did with the seat. So the four doors lower the first ask to one sentence: here are your own elected officials, and here is what they voted for. From there the reader can look for somebody better to run in the primary, which is the cheapest way a captured incumbent loses a seat. That argument is laid out in [Replacing Captured Incumbents](/docs/replace_incumbents/overview).

The third column is what keeps a party door from turning into a campaign tool. Showing the strongest challenger who is not from your side, at the same photograph size and under the same arithmetic, is the movement paying for its own honesty on the surface where it would be cheapest to cheat. It is also why the neutral site still exists and still matters, which [Why A Non-Partisan Site](/docs/non_partisan_site/overview) covers directly.

## What keeps it honest

* **One pipeline, and it cannot be reached from a door.** The awards are computed once, party-blind, and the edition configuration has no field a threshold or a weight could be written into. A build check fails on any difference outside the permitted list.
* **The cross-party column is always on.** It is never suppressed to make a door's own side look better, and an empty seat gets a stated empty result rather than a missing column.
* **No comparative copy anywhere.** No door characterizes another party, compares parties, or attributes motive to one. The unit of judgement is a person and a vote.
* **The accent never touches a measurement.** Colour is chrome. An award bar means the same thing on all four doors.
* **No party endorsement, no fundraising, no campaign coordination.** The doors are a reading surface pointed at the official record. Every claim on one is a click from its source.
