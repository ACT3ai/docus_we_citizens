# Paid Supporter

Everything on We The Citizens is free, and stays free. A Paid Supporter is a citizen who pays a
monthly amount anyway, at a number they choose themselves. It buys exactly one thing: a members-only
room showing where the money went, what it bought, and what is being built next. It does not buy a
louder vote and it does not buy standing.

## What it does

Anyone can create an account for nothing and use the whole product. Becoming a **Supporter** is a
separate, optional step. The citizen picks how much to give from a ladder of monthly amounts — $40,
$100, $200, $400, $1,000 — or types a custom amount instead. There are no named plans. The **level
is simply the number** the citizen chose, and every supporter gets the same benefits.

Payment runs on the payment provider's own hosted page. There is no card field anywhere in the app,
and no card number ever reaches the movement's servers, which hold only the provider's identifiers
plus the card brand and last four digits for display.

Membership is granted by the provider's **webhook**, not by the browser coming back. The return trip
is a URL anybody could have typed, so it only refreshes the screen; the membership turns on when the
provider itself reports the charge succeeded. Nothing here is believed because a browser said so.

A card that fails once is treated as a **retry window**, not as somebody leaving. Benefits stay on
while the provider retries, a fix-payment line appears at the top of the page, and the membership
ends only when the subscription actually ends.

Stopping is reachable from every surface that touches money — the dashboard, the statements, the
payment-method page, and a plain sentence at the bottom of the invoices. The stop page is a
statement of consequences and nothing else: no further charges, benefits kept until the period
already paid for runs out, account and data untouched, resume any time. No guilt copy, no retention
offer, no survey wall.

Behind the door is **one members-only room**: where the money went, what it bought, what is being
built next, and the citizen's own membership block with links to manage or stop it, so the room that
takes the money is never more than two clicks from the way out. Its first version is small and
labelled as unfinished rather than padded with placeholder widgets.

One line governs what may ever go into it: **if withholding something from a free citizen would make
the product less honest, less usable, or less able to hold a politician to account, it does not
belong in that room.**

## The goal it serves

Hosting, data pipelines and AI runs cost money, and there are only so many ways to pay for them.
Advertising sells the reader's attention. A paywall sells access to the evidence. Selling citizens'
data sells the citizen. Each one makes somebody other than the citizen the customer, which is the
exact failure this project exists to document in
[money in politics](/docs/congress_for_citizens/money_in_politics).

A movement whose central charge is that money buys influence in politics cannot turn around and sell
influence inside itself. So the free line is drawn around all of the civic work — reading a record,
running the calculations, casting a vote, filing a proposal — because holding an official to account
cannot be something a citizen has to be able to afford. What the money buys instead is transparency
about the money.

There is also a plain reason the room exists at all. A movement that takes monthly money and then
shows its funders exactly the same page as everybody else is a movement that stops receiving monthly
money. Publishing that reasoning is the same instinct that runs through the rest of
[the movement](/docs/the_movement/overview): show the mechanism, including the parts that are not
flattering.

## What keeps it honest

* **Money is not an input to trust.** Supporter status does not move the
  [trust ladder](/docs/trust_scores/overview), does not weight a vote, and does not skip an approval
  step. That is not left to good intentions: a test shipped with the code asserts that toggling
  supporter status changes no citizen's trust score.
* **An admin who is not a Supporter does not get in either.** No staff back door, because a back
  door would make the room's own claim — this is what your money bought — impossible for a member to
  verify.
* **The entrance is an invitation, never a wall.** The Supporters Area appears in the navigation for
  every citizen, free or paying, never hidden and never greyed out with a padlock. A non-member's
  click lands on a page explaining what the room is and stating, in the same panel, that everything
  civic stays free for everyone.
* **Stated limits, in this version.** Refunds are not self-serve and go through support by email. The
  payment is not tax-deductible; this is not a charity. The room's contents are still being built,
  and the page says so rather than implying otherwise.
* **On your own machine it can be absent entirely.** An install with no payment keys configured
  renders the supporter pages with a plain note that payments are not configured here, and nothing
  else in the product notices.
