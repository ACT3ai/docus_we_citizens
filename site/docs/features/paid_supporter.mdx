# Paid Supporter

Everything on We The Citizens is free, and stays free. A Paid Supporter is a citizen who decides to
pay a monthly amount anyway, at a number they pick themselves. It buys exactly one thing: a
members-only room showing where the money went, what it bought, and what is being built next. It
does not buy a louder vote, and it does not buy standing.

## What it does

Anyone can create an account for nothing and use the whole product. Becoming a **Supporter** is a
separate, optional step. The citizen picks how much to give from a ladder of monthly amounts — $40,
$100, $200, $400, $1,000 — or types a custom amount of their own. There are no named plans with
different feature sets. The **level is simply the number** the citizen chose.

Payment runs on the payment provider's own hosted page. There is no card field anywhere in the app,
no card library loaded in the browser, and no card number ever reaches the movement's servers. The
app stores identifiers and a card's brand and last four digits for display, and nothing else.

Membership is granted by the provider's **webhook**, not by the browser coming back. The return trip
from the payment page is a URL anybody could type, so it only refreshes the screen; the membership
itself turns on when the provider reports that the charge succeeded. Nothing here is believed
because a browser said it.

A card that fails once is treated as a **retry window**, not as somebody leaving. Benefits stay on
while the provider retries, with a fix-payment line at the top of the page. They end when the
subscription actually ends.

Stopping is reachable from every surface that touches money — the dashboard, the statements page,
the payment method page, and a plain line at the bottom of the invoices page whose first sentence is
the link. The stop page is a statement of consequences and nothing else: no further charges, you
stay a Supporter until the period you already paid for runs out, your account and data are
untouched, you can resume any time. No guilt copy, no retention offer, no survey wall.

Behind the door is **one members-only room**. It shows where the money went, what it bought, and
what is being built next, and it carries the citizen's own membership block with links to manage or
stop it — so the room that takes the money is never more than two clicks from the way out. The room
is small in its first version and says so on the page rather than filling itself with placeholder
widgets.

The rule governing what may ever go in there is one line: **if withholding something from a free
citizen would make the product less honest, less usable, or less able to hold a politician to
account, it does not belong in that room.** It goes on the free surface instead.

## The goal it serves

Hosting, data pipelines and AI runs cost real money, and there are only a few ways to pay for them.
Advertising sells the reader's attention. A paywall sells access to the evidence. Selling citizens'
data sells the citizen. Each of those makes somebody other than the citizen the customer, which is
the exact failure this project exists to document in
[Congress](/docs/congress_for_citizens/money_in_politics).

A movement whose central charge is that money buys influence in politics cannot turn around and sell
influence inside itself. That is why the free line is drawn at the civic work and not one inch
closer: reading a record, running the calculations, casting a vote and filing a proposal are free
forever, because the ability to hold an official to account cannot be something a citizen has to
afford. What money buys instead is transparency about the money. That is the only product on the
paid side of the line.

There is a plain reason the room exists at all. A movement that takes monthly money and then shows
its funders exactly the same page as everybody else stops receiving monthly money. Saying that out
loud is better than pretending the payment is a donation into a void, and it is the same instinct
that runs through the rest of [the movement](/docs/the_movement/overview): publish the mechanism,
including the unflattering parts.

## What keeps it honest

* **Money is not an input to trust.** Supporter status does not move the
  [trust ladder](/docs/trust_scores/overview), does not weight a vote, and does not skip an
  approval step. This is not left to good intentions: a test shipped with the code reads the trust
  engine's own source and fails the build if that engine so much as mentions supporter status.
* **An admin who is not a Supporter does not get in either.** There is no staff back door, because a
  back door would make the room's own claim — this is what your money bought — impossible for a
  member to verify.
* **The entrance is an invitation, never a wall.** The Supporters Area appears in the navigation for
  every citizen, free or paying, never hidden and never greyed out. A click by a non-member goes to
  a page explaining what the room is and that everything civic stays free, which is a sentence that
  only works when both halves are true.
* **What it does not do yet, stated plainly.** Refunds are not self-serve in this version; they go
  through support by email. The room's first version is mostly a statement of intent. And the
  payment is not tax-deductible — this is not a charity, and no page will imply otherwise.
* **On your own machine it can be absent entirely.** An install with no payment keys configured
  renders every supporter page with a plain note that payments are not configured, and nothing else
  in the product notices or cares.
