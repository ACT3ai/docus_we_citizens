# The Trust Ladder

Anyone can open We The Citizens, read everything it publishes, record their own positions, build their own evidence chains, and run the whole calculation on their own machine. None of that needs permission from anybody. What has to be earned is influence over *other people* — publishing into a shared space, being counted in a community's consensus, vouching for somebody else. The trust ladder is the five-rung path to that, and every rung is climbed by doing things anyone can check.

## What it does

The **capability ladder** has five rungs: New, Identified, Contributing, Established, Steward. Five and no more, because you have to be able to read your own standing in ten seconds. You always see the checklist: which items you have met, and what the next rung needs.

**Level 1 is the whole individual product.** Recording your positions, writing a value system, building an evidence chain, generating a proposed law, running the calculation over your own work — none of it is locked. Every gate sits on acting on other people, never on your own reasoning.

Rungs are earned by information you hand over, not by money, invitation, or an admin's favor:

* **An anchor** — a verified email, or a proven account elsewhere. Proving you control a pseudonymous account somewhere else satisfies this on its own, so the anchor never costs you your anonymity.
* **Work the install can actually read** — published records that pass their schema, a public repository of your own reasoning, an evidence chain with its gaps named, a value system that parses. Structural validity only: nobody can block a rung by calling your work low quality, because that power does not exist.
* **Time and steadiness** — account age, and activity spread across months rather than one burst.
* **Vouches** from citizens already at Established or above. A vouch is scarce — ten outstanding at most — withdrawable with no explanation, and non-transitive: being vouched for does not let you vouch. A batch of fresh accounts cannot lift each other up, because standing comes from your position among people already verified as real.

Consensus weighting is flat at the top on purpose. Level 1 counts zero, Level 2 counts half, and Levels 3, 4 and 5 all count the same. The ladder keeps sockpuppets out of a count; it does not make some citizens matter more than others.

Behind the ladder, and separate from it, sits a continuous 0–100 **trust score**. That subsystem is admin-facing. It is not published to citizens, never shown to one citizen about another, and it gates nothing — gates read the rung. Opening any part of it is a specified multi-stage path with written prerequisites, and that path has not been taken.

## The goal it serves

A platform where citizens document the capture of their own government will be attacked. Spam, astroturf accounts, impersonation and deliberate poisoning of a community's consensus are the expected cost of doing this in public. The obvious defense is a login wall, and the charter refuses it: reading the record must never require an account, because a movement that demands identity at the door has already lost the people most at risk from giving it.

The ladder is the alternative. Trust buys attention and weight; it never buys authority, and nothing is auto-approved because a trusted account submitted it. That rule is what stops a well-funded operation buying standing here the way influence is bought in Washington — see [money in politics](/docs/congress_for_citizens/money_in_politics) for the problem we refuse to reproduce.

It has to stay honest in the other direction too. Influence earned in public is capped on purpose, because unbounded merit becomes a clique within a few election cycles — [the rules that keep it honest](/docs/meritocracy/the_rules_that_keep_it_honest) covers those caps, and [the mesh of verified citizens](/docs/trust_scores/the_mesh_and_what_it_defends_against) covers what it is defending.

A pseudonymous citizen is never capped. Every criterion is satisfiable without publishing a real name, all the way to the top rung. The claim being proven is *one real person*, never *we know who you are*.

## What keeps it honest

* **The measurement is about our evidence, not about you.** A brand-new, honest citizen sits low on the ladder, and the product is required to say so in those words — we have little evidence about this account yet — never "low-trust user." That is the difference between a measurement and an accusation.
* **Karma never feeds believability.** Doing a great deal of admired work is not evidence that an account is a real, distinct human, and counting it would let a busy sockpuppet farm buy its way in on volume.
* **Money buys nothing, and that is tested.** Supporter status is asserted in code not to be an input. Nothing about a rung is purchasable, ever.
* **Verification proves control, not personhood.** Two people can share one account; a staffer can run a channel. The product states that limit rather than claiming one account per human.
* **Staying at Level 1 forever is legitimate.** It is a complete way to use this product, and the interface is forbidden from nagging a quiet citizen into climbing.
