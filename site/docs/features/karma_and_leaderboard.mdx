# Karma And The History Book

You do something real — knock doors, file a sourced record, get a challenger to agree to run — and
the movement records it, has another citizen confirm it, and keeps it. **Karma** is the name for the
recognition that work earns. It is the citizen's own score for their own logged work, which is a
different thing from an award the movement publishes about a public official, and it is deliberately
kept in different words.

## What it does

An action is **recorded** the moment a citizen logs it, and **scored** later, once somebody else has
confirmed it. The citizen sees "recorded" immediately and "worth N karma" after verification. The gap
between the two is the verification lifecycle, and it is left visible rather than hidden.

Every kind of action lives in a published catalog. Each entry carries raw points, a **track** — the
lane of work it belongs to, such as Challengers, Communications, Organizing or Lawmaking — a
**verification tier** running from automatic through a peer to a community steward to an admin, a
monthly cap, and a flag marking the few outcomes that actually changed something. A challenger filing
to run is not the same size as a post, and the catalog says so in advance rather than after the fact.

Raw points are treated as noise on purpose. They are spiky, and they reward whoever happens to have
the most peers nearby to report on them. So at month close a single pass sums raw points per citizen
per track, damps repetition of the same action, normalizes each track against that month's active
cohort, weights the tracks so the loudest lane is not the only lane worth being in, applies an
integrity factor, and floors any month that contains a decisive act. What comes out is one **monthly
karma score**, and monthly scores accumulate into a **lifetime total**.

The public boards are headed *History Book of Citizens Fixing Democracy*. Several time windows run
side by side, because recent work and lifetime work are different stories: today that is the last
three months, the last two weeks, and lifetime, with a 24-hour board designed but not yet built
because the server does not compute that window. A rail follows whatever row the reader points at and
says who that person is. A citizen outside the visible rows is told their true rank over the whole
window instead of being faked into a position.

Every month is itemised in a statement the citizen can check:

* every verified act, with its base points and what it was awarded
* a plain-words line under every cap and every multiplier, so no adjustment is a silent difference
  between two totals
* a separate section for the acts that did **not** count — pending, rejected, or disputed — each with
  its reason
* the arithmetic track by track, ending at the single monthly number
* the policy exactly as it ran that month, not today's policy
* a *Dispute this item* action on a single line

## The goal it serves

Positions are cheap and easy to collect. A doorknock, a sourced record filed against the official
record, an hour of recorded testimony, a challenger who agreed to run — those are scarce, and nothing
asked ordinary citizens for them or kept a receipt when they were given. This exists to make the
movement's scarce resource countable.

Credit here is something other people give you. A citizen cannot verify their own completion, and the
actions worth the most cannot be self-logged at all — they arrive as a report filed by somebody else.
Every line traces back to a URL and a description a stranger can open. That is what separates a record
of work from a follower count.

It is also the lever. Replacing a captured incumbent is not won by argument; it is won by volume of
unglamorous, checkable work done before a primary, by people who mostly do not know each other. A
movement that cannot see who is doing that work cannot ask the right person for the right thing. The
[action catalog](/docs/take_action/the_action_catalog) is the menu, the
[strategy for replacing captured incumbents](/docs/replace_incumbents/overview) is what the menu is
for, and karma is the ledger connecting the two.

Underneath the daily boards sits the **Leader History Book**: a permanent, hash-chained record of the
contributions that changed something, and the citizens who made them happen. An entry is about a
contribution, not a personality. Nobody arrives there automatically — a steward nominates and an admin
confirms — and once an entry is anchored it cannot be quietly rewritten.

## What keeps it honest

* **No negative karma, no downvotes, no public shaming.** Citizens report each other's work
  positively. The only downward force is the rejection of a claim nobody can support, and a rejection
  records its reason instead of deleting the record.
* **The anti-gaming rules are stated, not implied.** You cannot verify your own work. One URL can back
  one record. Work above a monthly cap is still kept in the history but is worth zero and is marked as
  capped. Two citizens verifying each other repeatedly in one month are flagged for review, and the
  flag is informational rather than an automatic penalty.
* **A sealed month is final.** After the seal, a correction is appended beside the original as an
  amendment with its own anchored block. The original is never overwritten.
* **The board publishes nothing a citizen did not publish.** The privacy contract is enforced on the
  server, not in the browser: the display name is the one the citizen chose, and no photograph is
  delivered unless they turned one on. A fully pseudonymous citizen can hold the top of every board,
  and nothing on the page nudges them to identify themselves in order to be there.
* **It says what it is not.** Karma measures logged work for this movement and nothing else. It is not
  a measure of a person's worth, it is never a finding about a politician, and a citizen with no karma
  is a citizen who has not logged anything here.
