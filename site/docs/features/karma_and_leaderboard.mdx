# Karma And The History Book

You do something real — knock doors, file a sourced record, get a challenger to agree to run — and
the movement records it, has another citizen confirm it, and keeps it. **Karma** is the
recognition that work earns: a citizen's own score for their own logged work, kept in different
words from an award the movement publishes about a public official.

## What it does

An action is **recorded** the moment a citizen logs it, and **scored** later, once somebody else has
confirmed it. The citizen sees "recorded" at once and "worth N karma" after verification. The gap
between the two is left visible rather than hidden.

Every kind of action lives in a published catalog. Each entry carries raw points, a **track** — the
lane of work it belongs to, such as Challengers, Communications, Organizing or Lawmaking — a
**verification tier** running from automatic to peer to steward to admin, a monthly cap, and a flag
for the few outcomes that changed something. A challenger filing to run is not the same size as a
post, and the catalog says so in advance.

Raw points are noise on purpose: they land unevenly and reward whoever has the most peers nearby to
report on them, which measures social density, not contribution. So at month close one pass sums raw
points per track, damps repetition of the same action, normalizes each track against that month's
active cohort, weights the tracks so the loudest lane is not the only one that counts, applies an
integrity factor, and floors any month containing a decisive act. Out comes one **monthly karma
score**, and monthly scores accumulate into a **lifetime total**.

The public boards are headed *History Book of Citizens Fixing Democracy*. Several time windows run
side by side, because recent work and lifetime work are different stories: the last three months,
the last two weeks, and lifetime. A 24-hour board is designed but not built: the server does not
compute that window. A citizen outside the visible rows is told their true rank over the whole
window rather than being faked into a position.

Every month is itemized in a statement the citizen can check: each verified act with its base points
and what it was awarded; a plain-words line under every cap and multiplier, so no adjustment is
silent; a section for the acts that did **not** count — pending, rejected, or disputed — each with
its reason; the arithmetic track by track; the policy as it ran that month rather than today's; and
a *Dispute this item* action on a single line.

## The goal it serves

Opinions are cheap. A doorknock, a sourced record, an hour of recorded testimony, a challenger who
agreed to run — those are scarce, and nothing in our politics asked citizens for them or kept a
receipt. This exists to make that scarce resource countable.

Credit here is something other people give you: the actions worth the most cannot be self-logged at
all, and arrive as a report filed by somebody else. Every line traces back to a URL a stranger can
open. That is what separates a record of work from a follower count.

It is also the lever. A captured incumbent is not removed by argument; the seat changes hands on the
volume of unglamorous, checkable work done before a primary, and a movement that cannot see who is
doing that work cannot ask the right person for the right thing.
The [action catalog](/docs/take_action/the_action_catalog) is the menu,
[replacing captured incumbents](/docs/replace_incumbents/overview) is what the menu is for, and
karma is the ledger connecting the two.

Underneath the daily boards sits the **Leader History Book**: a permanent, hash-chained record of
the contributions that changed something and the citizens behind them. An entry is about a
contribution, not a personality. Nobody arrives automatically — a steward nominates, an admin
confirms — and an anchored entry cannot be quietly rewritten.

## What keeps it honest

* **No negative karma, no downvotes, no public shaming.** The only downward force is rejecting a
  claim nobody can support, and a rejection records its reason instead of deleting the record.
* **The anti-gaming rules are stated, not implied.** You cannot verify your own work. One URL backs
  one record. Work above a monthly cap is still kept in the history, worth zero and marked capped. Two
  citizens verifying each other repeatedly in one month are flagged for review rather than
  automatically penalized.
* **A sealed month is final.** A later correction is appended beside the original as an amendment
  with its own anchored block, showing before and after. The original is never overwritten.
* **The board publishes nothing a citizen did not publish.** The privacy contract is enforced on the
  server, not in the browser: the board renders the display name the citizen chose, and no photograph
  unless they turned one on. A fully pseudonymous citizen can hold the top of every board.
* **It says what it is not.** Karma measures logged work for this movement and nothing else. It is not
  a measure of a person's worth, and it is never a finding about a politician.
