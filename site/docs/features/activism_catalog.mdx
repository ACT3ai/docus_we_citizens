# Actions You Can Actually Take

This is the part of the app that answers "and then what". You have read the records and you agree:
here is a wall of things worth doing, each with instructions and a place to write down what you did,
with a link somebody else can check. It exists because a movement that can explain the problem and
cannot tell you what to do this week is a reading group.

## What it does

The menu is **data, not software**. One file in the public data repository describes one action: a
label, a summary, its track, a time estimate, a difficulty, why it matters, an ordered how-to list,
what evidence to bring back, the cautions that bite hardest for that action, and what it is worth in
karma. Adding a file adds a card, with no software release.

Actions run across four **tracks** — challengers, communications, organizing and lawmaking — and the
menu is a wall of cards rather than a table on purpose. A table asks you to compare. This page's job
is to make one card look worth doing tonight.

Each card leads to a walkthrough written in the imperative, for somebody who has already decided. The
**cautions come first**, above the fold, because the rules that matter are the ones you need before
you post, not after. The steps render as a checklist you tick as you work in another tab; it lives in
your own browser and is never sent anywhere. A button follows you down the page to the log form.

**Logging** records one thing one person did in the world: who did it, what kind of action it was,
when it happened, a one-line title, a description of what *changed* rather than what you did, and one
or more links. Links are stored exactly as you typed them, and no crawler is sent out to read them. A
link that does not answer earns a warning and an offer to archive it, never a block. The preferred
path is a **peer report**, filing on somebody else's behalf: nobody verifies their own work.

Above the submit row sits the **legality gate**, and it is two separate things. First a required
question with nothing preselected, because a pre-checked "yes" is an answer the app gave on your
behalf. Then, beside the button, an attestation in your own voice that the action was legal and
ethical — never sticky, never restored from a draft, never settable by a link.

## The goal it serves

Positions are cheap and this app was already good at collecting them. What the movement is short of
is action — doorknocks, filings, recorded testimony, a challenger who agreed to run. The failure this
closes is the citizen who reads three problem laws, agrees with all of them, and closes the tab. So
the answer to "and then what" works signed out, without a community and without an account.

The second reason matters more. This movement's argument is that the people in power are the ones
breaking the rules, and that argument only holds if we do not. So the boundary is built into the form
rather than bolted on as a moderation policy. The app records that a citizen acted; it never assigns,
schedules or dispatches a named person to a named place. And it refuses to keep the one file most
useful to its opponents: a movement that stores its members' self-incrimination has built the
evidence locker for the people it is opposing.

Why any of this is necessary is argued elsewhere:
[The Movement](/the_movement/overview) for what joining means,
[Great American Ethics](/american_ethics/overview) for the standard being restored, and
[Take Action](/take_action/overview) for the catalog as a citizen meets it.

## What keeps it honest

* **The prohibited list is specific and published**, not a vague appeal to good conduct: violence or
  threats, property damage, intimidation or harassment of an individual, doxxing, election
  interference with no "they did it first" exception, impersonation, fabricated media, unlawful
  access to records, and anything unlawful where you are. Criticism of a public official's public
  conduct is not harassment. Showing up at their house is.
* **What is accepted is stated just as plainly**, so the list above is not read as timidity: speaking,
  writing, filming, publishing, canvassing, petitioning, calling and visiting offices, testifying,
  records requests, lawful poll watching, recruiting candidates, boycotting and lawful assembly.
  Inconvenient to powerful people is not a disqualifier. Unlawful is.
* **Answering the gate the wrong way transmits nothing.** No request, no draft, no telemetry, no log
  line, and the selection is never stored.
* **The attestation records the policy you actually saw**, by version and by hash, so what a citizen
  agreed to stays what they agreed to. No IP address, no browser fingerprint, no location.
* **A claim about a named living person carrying no link goes to review.** State what somebody did,
  with a source. Never what they are.
* **What is running, and what is not.** The menu, the cards, the walkthrough pages and the stored
  record are built. The standing campaigns band, the public action feed and the dedicated log page
  with this gate wired into it are specified and not yet shipped; an action is logged today through
  the karma form.
