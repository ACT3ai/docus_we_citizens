# Who Should Run, And Where

Citizens recommend people they think belong in office. Over a year that produces a long list of names with nothing next to any of them, and a list of names is not a plan. This is the run that turns it into one — a named challenger per seat, published with the arithmetic that put them there and with a stated reason beside every name that did not get a seat.

## What it does

It reads **the approved roster and nothing else**. A citizen's recommendation is a request, not an entry — it is invisible to this run until an admin approves it. Otherwise anyone could put a name on the movement's slate by filling in a form.

For each person on that roster it keeps **three numbers that are never collapsed into one**. How they came out against the published qualification areas. **How much the movement actually knows about them**, published as "64% known" rather than buried. And **office readiness** — how senior an office their record shows they are prepared to hold, which is a statement about level, not about virtue. Someone can be well known and thinly qualified, or strongly qualified with no executive record at all.

Citizens separately cast one to five stars against **eight named offices**, from state assembly and mayor up to president. An **office fit** combines those stars with how far the person's readiness sits from what that office demands. Being a strong fit for a state assembly seat and a weak fit for a governorship is a normal, respectable result, and the page says it that way.

What puts people in order for a slot is **the combined qualification result at the movement's declared weights, and nothing else**. Home state, party, seat kind and constitutional eligibility act only as filters — they decide whether a person is in the contest, never how high they come out of it.

Then the seats are walked from the most senior downward, and **every position carries both major-party slots**. At each open slot the highest-placed eligible person takes it, and one person takes one seat. A seat's demanded readiness is a **soft floor**: someone placed under it is recorded as a **stretch** with the shortfall named. Naming the stretch is the point.

Every name that did not get a slot comes out with a reason attached — runner-up by a stated margin, held back for an unsourced claim or a constitutional bar, or genuinely out of open slots. Nobody is deleted. **An unfilled slot is published as a recruiting signal**, not an error: "no Democrat contender for this position" is exactly what the movement needs to see.

## The goal it serves

The movement's hardest question is not *who is bad?* The voting record already answers that one. It is **who should we run, and where?** — and that question is normally settled in a room the public never sees, by people whose reasoning is never written down. A slate arrived at that way cannot be argued with, only accepted or ignored.

A captured incumbent is replaced at an election and nowhere else, and the way that fails is familiar: three plausible challengers split the same voters, the incumbent takes the primary with a third of the vote, and everyone agrees afterwards that somebody should have stood aside. Deciding *before* the primary which one name a seat gets behind is the whole lever. This run makes that decision in the open, on weights published in advance, so a visitor to their own district page can interrogate it instead of trusting it. See [Replacing Captured Incumbents](/docs/replace_incumbents/overview) and [Good New Leaders](/docs/new_politicians/overview).

It also keeps the decision honest about level. A good organizer with no executive record is not improved by being pointed at a governorship. The ladder is how *"this person is better suited to a state assembly seat"* becomes arithmetic instead of an editor's instinct — the same reason influence inside this movement is [earned in public and capped](/docs/meritocracy/overview) rather than handed out.

## What keeps it honest

* **The wall holds.** Nothing this run computes can ever move a Monkey Award or a Llama Award — those two are fed by the in-office voting record only. What comes out of here is published as the Flamingo Award, and a person who holds both kinds carries them side by side.
* **A number with no traceable evidence is rejected**, not published with a caveat. A person the movement cannot yet trace falls to the Flamingo's **unknown** state with a stated cause — never to the "assessed and nothing earned" state, and never to 0%.
* **Every placement is published with its known percentage.** A placement is a stronger claim than an assessment, so the anti-overclaim pairing matters more here, not less.
* **An unfilled seat is a finding.** The run prints the threshold and the best result that failed it, because a threshold nobody can see is a threshold nobody can argue with.
* **The run does not publish itself.** It seals a dated edition and a difference against last quarter; a human reads that difference and commits it, and the commit is what "approved" means. Anyone may run a throwaway simulation at any time, and no simulation can move what visitors see.
* **A change between quarters carries its reason.** Moving a person from governor to a House seat is stated as what it usually is — a fact about who else entered the pool — never as a judgment about them.
