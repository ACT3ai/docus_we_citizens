# The Flamingo Award

Most of the people worth electing have never held office. There is no roll call to read on them, so the only checkable evidence is what they have said in public and what can be sourced about their background. The Flamingo Award is what the movement gives for that evidence, and it is the only award with a state meaning *we have not looked yet*.

## What it does

The Flamingo reads a person from their own recorded words and from what can be sourced about them: transcriptions of interviews and appearances where the person is speaking, background research, resume, education, and public commitments, plus two layers of citizen stars — one from trusted citizens, one from citizens generally. It has no reader for a roll call, an authorship, or a committee act. That code is not switched off; it is not there.

The evidence is sorted into eighteen **qualification areas** — the named things that make somebody worth electing. Each is read on its own, from individual claims up to the person, then the eighteen are combined at weights published in advance that sum to one hundred. Adding a nineteenth would mean re-pricing the other eighteen, in public.

One resolver then decides which of three states the person is in, and it checks the empty case first. No number at all is **unknown**. At or above the published threshold is **earned**, at the percentage computed. Too little known to make an adverse finding is **unknown** again. Only what is left is **not earned**.

* **Earned** renders the full-colour flamingo beside the percentage of the award given. A 36% Flamingo Award is an award, given in the percentage earned.
* **Not earned** renders a solid dark red fill with no bird: we had enough, we did the arithmetic, and no part of the award was earned. It is reserved for a finding of harm, not for a middling result.
* **Unknown** renders a solid gray-brown fill with no bird: we do not know enough to say yet. That is a statement about us, not a claim about the person.

The two blank states are different facts and are never collapsed into one another. Neither ever renders as 0%, because a zero would put a state back onto a scale. A blank carries an em dash, a typed cause in plain words — no videos yet, no transcript yet, not assessed yet, or they did not discuss this — and where one exists, the action that would close the gap. In all three states the page also prints how much of the person is known, as "N% known".

## The goal it serves

The movement's strategy is elections: get a party's own base behind the strongest challenger before the primary rather than splitting three ways after it. A challenger has no voting record, so the ordinary way a voter sizes one up is party label, name recognition, and whoever bought the most advertising — exactly the levers money in politics already controls. The Flamingo exists so there is something else to look at: the person's own words, against rubrics anyone can read first, published with the quotes that moved the result. See [Good New Leaders](/docs/new_politicians/overview) for who is on that roster and [Qualifications](/docs/qualifications/overview) for what they are read against.

The unknown state exists for the second reason. The cheapest way to damage a newcomer nobody has heard of is to publish silence about them and let the reader fill it in. If our own gap in evidence rendered the same as a finding, this would be a smear machine with arithmetic attached. So the gap is typed, named, coloured differently, and pointed at a queue of work.

Third, the unit of judgement stays a person, not a party. Nothing in the eighteen areas asks who somebody caucuses with. A reader who wants the partisan version goes to their own party's door; the assessment underneath is the same either way. See [Replacing Captured Incumbents](/docs/replace_incumbents/overview) for how it gets used.

## What keeps it honest

* **The wall is enforced in code, not in style.** A build test fails if a voting-record term ever reaches a Flamingo result, and the reverse is equally a defect.
* **Red is only for big problems.** The threshold is published, and everything from it upward is conferred at the percentage computed. A middling reading gets an award at that percentage; it does not get the red fill.
* **No adverse finding off almost nothing.** A floor on how much is known guards the not-earned branch only, and never withholds an award that was computed — the honest answer to a thin record is to publish the number with "N% known" beside it.
* **The coverage gap is published as a finding about us.** Every run reports how many people landed on each cause of a blank, and an area nobody has been assessed in is named as a hole in our own rubric authoring.
* **An award decides nothing on its own.** It does not endorse anyone and never triggers a transition automatically. It is an input to a citizen vote, never a substitute for one.
