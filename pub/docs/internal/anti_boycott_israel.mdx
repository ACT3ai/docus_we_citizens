# Anti-Boycott Israel Laws

These are the Israel boycott laws that remove rights from American citizens who participate in the BDS movement.

## What Is BDS?

BDS stands for Boycott, Divestment, and Sanctions. It is a Palestinian-led, nonviolent movement launched in 2005 that calls for economic boycotts, divestment from companies, and international sanctions against Israel. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott,_Divestment_and_Sanctions))

Its stated goals are to pressure Israel to comply with international law by:

* Ending its occupation and settlement activity in the Palestinian territories (West Bank, East Jerusalem, and Gaza).
* Granting full equality to Arab-Palestinian citizens of Israel.
* Respecting the right of Palestinian refugees to return to their homes and properties (as outlined in UN resolutions). ([bdsmovement.net](https://bdsmovement.net))

The movement is coordinated by the Palestinian BDS National Committee and is modeled after the anti-apartheid campaign against South Africa. Supporters describe it as a human-rights and anti-racism effort aimed at ending what they call Israel's "oppression" and "apartheid" policies. ([bdsmovement.net](https://bdsmovement.net))

## Critics of BDS

Critics — including many Jewish organizations, pro-Israel groups, and governments — argue that BDS delegitimizes Israel as a Jewish state, promotes a one-state solution that would end Israel's Jewish majority and character, and is antisemitic in effect (even if not always in intent). They point out that full implementation of its demands would replace the two-state solution with a single state in which Jews would be a minority. ([jcrc.org](https://www.jcrc.org))

## Anti-BDS Laws

Anti-BDS bills (also called anti-boycott laws) are laws passed primarily in US states that penalize individuals, companies, or organizations that participate in boycotts of Israel. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-BDS_legislation))

**In the United States:** As of 2024, 38 states have enacted such measures via legislation or executive order. These laws typically do one or both of the following:

* Prohibit state agencies from signing contracts with companies that boycott Israel (or, in some cases, Israeli settlements).
* Require public pension funds or investment vehicles to divest from or avoid entities engaged in BDS activity.

Examples include Texas's HB 89 (signed by Gov. Greg Abbott), which bars state contracts with BDS participants, and similar laws in Illinois (the first to pass one), New York, Florida, and others. ([gov.texas.gov](https://gov.texas.gov))

**Federal level:** Congress has passed non-binding resolutions condemning BDS (e.g., House Resolution 246 in 2019) and considered bills like the Israel Anti-Boycott Act and the more recent IGO Anti-Boycott Act (H.R. 867, reintroduced in 2025). The IGO version extends existing anti-boycott rules to boycotts initiated by international organizations such as UN bodies. ([congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov))

## Legal Challenges

These laws enjoy broad bipartisan support in many states but have faced legal challenges. Opponents — including the ACLU — argue they violate the First Amendment because political boycotts are protected speech and expressive conduct. Several court rulings have struck down or limited specific state laws on free-speech grounds; the US Supreme Court has not issued a definitive nationwide ruling yet. ([Palestine Legal](https://legislation.palestinelegal.org))

## Summary

BDS is a global campaign using economic pressure against Israel over Palestinian rights. Anti-BDS bills are the counter-measures governments (especially US states) use to refuse to do business with boycotters. The debate centers on whether BDS is legitimate protest or illegitimate discrimination and delegitimization.

## Why This Matters for We The Citizens

Politicians who vote YES on anti-BDS laws are voting to restrict the First Amendment rights of American citizens to engage in peaceful economic protest. This is a searchable voting record pattern.
